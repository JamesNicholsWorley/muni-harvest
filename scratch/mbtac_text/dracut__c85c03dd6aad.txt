What if we remain non-compliant? 
Per Chapter 40A Section 3A - An MBTA community that fails to comply with this 
section shall not be eligible for funds from the Housing Choice Initiative; the Local 
Capital Projects Fund;  the MassWorks infrastructure; and the HousingWorks 
infrastructure programs. 
In Attorney General v. Milton, 495 Mass. 183 (2025), the Supreme Judicial Court 
conﬁrmed that the Act is constitutional, that communities must comply, and that the 
Attorney General may enforce against noncompliance. 
In June 2025, the Superior Court determined that the Act is not an “unfunded 
mandate”. 
MBTA Communities that fail to comply risk lawsuit under federal and state fair 
housing laws.  
As of July 2025, non-compliant communities may lose discretionary state funding.

Would a developer need to buy out the current occupants to redevelop an area? 
Generally yes, an applicant for building permit needs to show they own or have owner’s 
permission to develop.  In the case of a condo complex, such permission of the ‘owner’ would 
require purchase of the units as spelled out in the Master Deed. 
What would be the anticipated increase in local residential tax revenue for potential new units? 
The average home tax bill in Dracut is currently just over $5,000, we can’t predict what will be 
constructed or what actual revenue could be. 
Is there or has there been any other towns where development occurred where they just 
claimed the zone developed as eminent domain?    
Eminent Domain requires public beneﬁt and payment for the appraised value of land taken.  Such 
needs to be approved at Town Meeting. 
If the developments where to be built who would monitor the 10% that are required to be 
aƯordable?  How are rental or sale prices set? 
NMCOG, our regional planning authority, has a new Housing Services OƯice that is intended to 
exactly this - https://www.nmcog.org/301/Northern-Middlesex-Regional-Housing-Serv.  Deeds 
are tracked prior to and following sales and rental properties are monitored to ensure that they 
meet State criteria.  The State caps are based on income, family size and location on an annual 
basis.
