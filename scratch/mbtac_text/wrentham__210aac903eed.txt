Key sections include:
Purpose: Encourage a variety of housing types,
 support vibrant neighborhoods, reduce vehicula
r travel, improve public health, and suppo
rt economic development through increa
sed housing density near public transit and 
businesses.
District Establishment: The MCMOD is a 85.8-acre
 overlay district divided into several sub-districts
, each with different uses and dimensional 
standards.
Permitted Uses: The bylaw allows multi-family
 housing and mixed-use developments as of right. Mixed-use developments can include retail, restaurants, offices, and residential units, among other uses .
Definitions: Terms such as affordable housing,
 multi-family housing, and mixed-use developmen
t are defined in detail. Affordable housing uni
ts must meet the criteria for inclusion in the 
Subsidized Housing Inventory (SHI).
Dimensional Standards: The bylaw provides
 specific guidelines for lot size, building height
, density, setbacks, and lot coverage for each su
b-district. It allows exceptions for renewable energy 
installations.
Parking Standards: Maximum parking
 requirements are outlined, and development
s are required to include bicycle parking and 
electric vehicle (EV) charging stations.
Affordability Requirements: At least 10% of the
 units in multi-family developments must b
e affordable to households earning 80% or less 
of the area median income. Developers have 
the option to contribute a fee in lieu of providing 
affordable units.
Design and Development Standards: The
 document details standards for site design
, open space, parking, landscaping, and buildi
ng placement to ensure aesthetically pleasi
ng, sustainable developments that integrate well 
into the surrounding area.
Site Plan Review: Multi-family or mixed-use
 projects with more than 25 units require sit
e plan review. Submission requirements and 
procedures for review are provided.
Decision and Recording: The Permit Granting
 Authority must issue a decision on application
s within 180 days, and approved permits must 
be recorded with the Norfolk County Registry of 
Deeds.
The bylaw aims to promote diverse housing
 options, sustainability, and public health b
y encouraging higher-density residenti
al developments close to public transportation 
and businesses.
