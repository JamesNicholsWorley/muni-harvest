Originally, the following funding sources were made “unavailable” to 
communities that failed to comply with Section 3A requirements: 
• the Housing Choice Initiative as described by the governor in a 
message to the general court dated December 11, 2017;  
•  the Local Capital Projects Fund established in section 2EEEE of 
chapter 29; and  
•  the MassWorks infrastructure program established in section 63 of 
chapter 23A. [Middleton is planning to use the proposed projects at 
the former Angelica’s to launch a future MassWorks project to 
address traffic concerns in the downtown area] 
 
In August 2023, the guidelines for Section 3A were amended and the 
following funding sources were added to the existing list of funding that 
will not be available to communities that fail to comply with Section 3A. 
“Section 3A provides that any MBTA community that fails to comply 
with Section 3A’s requirements will be ineligible for funding from any 
of the listed funding sources.  EOHLC will make determinations of 
compliance with Section 3A in accordance with these guidelines to 
inform state agency decisions on which MBTA communities are 
eligible to receive funding from the listed funding sources.    The 
following discretionary grant programs will take compliance with 
Section 3A into consideration when making grant award 
recommendations:  
1. Community Planning Grants, EOHLC, [Middleton received $75K to 
hire the planning consultant to assist us with rezoning efforts] 
2. Massachusetts Downtown Initiative, EOED,[Middleton plans to use 
this program in the near future to develop a design plan for the 
square that would enhance pedestrian/bicycle amenities] 
3. Urban Agenda, EOED, 
4. Rural and Small Town Development Fund, EOED, 
5. Brownfields Redevelopment Fund, MassDevelopment,[Middleton 
will need to apply for funds to help them market the property at 40 
School Street] 
6. Site Readiness Program, MassDevelopment, 
7. Underutilized Properties Program, MassDevelopment,
