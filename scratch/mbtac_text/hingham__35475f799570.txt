State Imposed Multifamily Zoning Requirement
2021 State Law Requires Zoning Capacity, Not Production
Section 3A of M.G.L. c. 40A (the Zoning Act) now requires that an MBTA community shall
have at least one zoning district of reasonable size in which multi-family housing is
permitted as of right and meets other criteria set forth in the statute, including:
• Minimum gross density of 15 units per acre;
• Not more than ½ miles from a commuter rail station, subway station, ferry terminal, or bus station, if 
applicable;
• No age restrictions; and
• Suitable for families with children.

Proposed Permitted Multi-family Housing Types & Updated Definitions
COMPLIANCE REQUIREMENTS
EXISTING BYLAW DEFINITIONS
Item 4 of Recommended Motion:
PROPOSED DEFINITIONS
Statutory Definition:  “Multi-family housing” 
means a building with 3 or more residential 
dwelling units or 2 or more buildings on the 
same lot with more than 1 residential dwelling 
unit in each building.
Multi-Unit Dwelling
A building intended and designed to contain three or more 
dwelling units.
Town House
A dwelling unit, attached by party walls on one or both 
sides to one or two other dwelling units, which has its own 
ground level entrance and front and rear yards.
No changes
Sample Bylaw (Comments on Definitions): 
A word or term that has an existing definition 
in the zoning bylaw or ordinance should not be 
given a different meaning in the overlay 
district. An existing definition may need to be 
modified to for the overlay district to be 
compliant with Section 3A.
Apartment House
A structure utilized wholly for residential dwelling units 
(and permitted uses accessory thereto) and containing 
three or more dwelling units.
Apartment House
A structure utilized wholly for residential dwelling units 
(and permitted uses accessory thereto) and containing 
threefour or more dwelling units.
Garden Apartment
A building designed to accommodate more than two 
dwelling units, with each entrance serving not more than 
four dwelling units, each of which extends through the 
building from front to rear.
Garden Apartment
A building consisting of not less than four nor more than 
ten connected dwelling units designed to accommodate
more than two dwelling units, with each entrance 
servicing not more than four dwelling units, each of which 
extends through the building from front to rear.
Two-Family Dwelling
A dwelling intended and designed to be occupied as two 
separate dwelling units. For all purposes in the By-law, 
two-family dwellings shall include attached garages, or 
other fully enclosed, attached structures, whether heated 
or unheated, connected to the primary structure.
Two-Family Dwelling
A dwelling intended and designed to be occupied as two 
separate dwelling units. For all purposes in the By-law, 
including the provisions set forth in Section III-H, two-
family dwellings shall include attached garages, or other 
fully enclosed, attached structures, whether heated or 
unheated, connected to the primary structure.

Proposed III-K, 9: Modifications/Exemptions for Statutory Compliance
COMPLIANCE REQUIREMENTS
EXISTING BYLAW
PROPOSED BYLAW LANGUAGE:
MODIFICATIONS/EXEMPTIONS FOR MBTA MULTIFAMILY 
HOUSING
Guidelines §4:  To comply with Section 3A, a multi-
family zoning district must allow multi-family housing 
“as of right,” meaning that the construction and 
occupancy of multi-family housing is allowed in that 
district without the need for a special permit, 
variance, zoning amendment, waiver, or other 
discretionary approval.
Existing Section I-C.2:
No C/Os for more than 85% of multifamily units 
before completion of site improvements.  
Subsection 2.b allows for relief from 85% 
limitation with SPA1 from ZBA. 
New Section III-K,9.a: A request pursuant to Section I-
C,2.b. of the By-Law shall not require a special permit 
and may be requested as a minor modification pursuant 
to Section I-I,9.b. of this By-Law. [Site plan review minor 
modification]
Guidelines Section 4.c.:  Zoning will not be deemed 
compliant with Section 3A’s requirement that multi-
family housing be allowed as of right if the zoning 
imposes requirements on multi-family housing that 
are not generally applicable to other uses.
Municipal Law Unit decision:  A town cannot require 
additional site-plan review documents for by-right 
multi-family that it does not require for other uses.
This affects two existing Site Plan Review provisions 
in Hingham’s existing By-Law.
Existing Section I-I,4.d:  “if the Planning Board 
or its designee, which may include its review 
consultants, have requested (no later than forty 
(40) days after receipt of the application) 
additional information or submittals from the 
applicant, such supplemental information shall 
be delivered no later than sixty (60) days after 
receipt of the application.”
New Section III-K,9.b(i): 
References in Section I-I,4.d. to additional information or 
submittals that may be required from the applicant shall 
be deemed to refer to missing or incomplete submittal 
requirements under Section I-I,5 and this Section V-K,9.
Existing Section I-I,5 (Submittal Requirements):
“k.  Such other materials necessary to enable 
the Planning Board to make a positive 
determination on the proposed project”
New Section III-K,9.b(ii):  
Section I-I,5.k shall not apply.

Section I-I.6c. 
“Character and Scale of Buildings
The design of the project shall minimize 
unreasonable departure from the character and 
scale of buildings in the vicinity or as previously 
existing on, or approved for, the site.”

case law: 
“Where ‘the specific area and use criteria stated 
in the by-law [are] satisfied, the [reviewing] 
board [does] not have discretionary power to

imposing reasonable terms and conditions on the 
proposed use.’”

proposed development so that its impact on each of 
the municipal services, ways, utilities and other 
resources does not exceed its existing design

proposed development regardless of the intensity of

By-Law.”
New Section III-K,9.b(iv): The second and third 
sentences in the introductory paragraph of Section I-
I,8. shall not apply.
