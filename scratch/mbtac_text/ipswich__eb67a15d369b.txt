ARTICLE __ Amend Zoning Bylaws, Add Section IX.V. Section 3A Multi-family Overlay District                    
To see if the Town will vote to: (1) amend its official zoning map by adding the Section 3A Multi-family Overlay 
District (MOD), consisting of four sub-districts, as delineated on a map shown below and entitled "Section 3A 
Multi-Family District (MOD), Town of Ipswich," and dated August, 2024; and (2) amend its Protective Zoning 
Bylaw by adding to Section IX. “Special Regulations” a new subsection "V", to read as follows:
 
“V. Section 3A Multi-family Overlay District 
A. 
Purpose 
The purpose of the Section 3A Multi-family Overlay District (MOD) is to allow multi-family housing as of right 
with site plan review in accordance with Section 3A of the Zoning Act (Massachusetts General Laws Chapter 
40A). This zoning provides for as of right with site plan review multi-family housing to accomplish the 
following purposes consistent with the Town of Ipswich Community Development Plan: 
1. Provide for additional housing type options, 
2. Increase the amount of affordable housing units in Ipswich; and 
3. Provide a residential base to support the commercial uses in Ipswich Town Center.

B. 
Establishment, Delineation and Applicability 
This MOD is an overlay district having a land area of approximately 54.9 acres that is superimposed over the 
underlying zoning district (s) and is shown on the Zoning Map. It is comprised of four sub-districts: MOD-1, 
MOD-2, MOD-3, and MOD-4. MOD-4 is a Mandatory Mixed-Use (MMU) development sub-district that 
requires non-residential uses on the ground floor. The MOD and its sub-districts are delineated on a map entitled 
"Section 3A Multi-Family Overlay District (MOD), Town of Ipswich" dated August 2024. This map is hereby 
made a part of the Official Zoning Map of the Town of Ipswich and is on file in the Office of the Town Clerk.

2. Underlying Zoning. The requirements for use, dimension, and all other provisions of the Zoning 
Bylaw governing the respective underlying zoning district(s) shall remain in full force, except for 
uses allowed as of right with site plan review or by special permit in the MOD. Uses that are not 
identified in this Section are governed by the requirements of the underlying zoning district(s). 
C. 
Definitions. 
For purposes of this Section IX.V., the following definitions shall apply. Section III. Definitions of this Zoning 
Bylaw shall also apply to this Section as applicable. Where definitions conflict, the definitions of this Section 
shall apply to the MOD. 
1. Affordable unit. A multi-family housing unit that is subject to a use restriction recorded in its 
chain of title limiting the sale price or rent or limiting occupancy to an individual or household of 
a specified income, or both. 
2. Affordable housing. Housing that contains Affordable Units as defined in Section IX.I.  
3. Applicant. A person, business, or organization that applies for a building permit, Site Plan 
Review, or Special Permit. 
4. As of right. Development that may proceed under the Zoning in place at time of application 
without the need for a special permit, variance, zoning amendment, waiver, or other discretionary 
zoning approval, and is subject to Site Plan Review for the purposes of this Section IX.V.  
5. Compliance Guidelines. Compliance Guidelines issued by the Commonwealth for Multi-Family 
Zoning Districts Under Section 3A of the Zoning Act as further revised or amended from time to 
time. 
6. Mixed-use development. Development containing a mix of residential uses and non- residential 
uses, including, commercial, institutional, industrial, or other uses.

7. Multi-family housing. A building with three or more residential dwelling units or two or more 
buildings on the same lot with more than one residential dwelling unit in each building. 
8. Multi-family zoning district. A zoning district, either a base district or an overlay district, in 
which multi-family housing is allowed as of right with site plan review.  
9. Sub-district. An area within the MOD that is geographically smaller than the MOD district and 
differentiated from the rest of the district by use, dimensional standards, or development standards 
set forth herein.

G. 
General Development Standards 
The following development standards in the MOD are applicable to all multi-family and mixed-use 
developments, which the Planning Board shall review, together with the standards described in Section X. “Site 
Plan Review,” to determine whether adequate provisions have been made by the Applicant to the maximum 
extent feasible. The Planning Board shall also adopt "Ipswich Multi-family Overlay District Design Guidelines" 
to provide additional guidance to Applicants and to the Board. At its discretion, the Planning Board may also 
refer a project to the Design Review Board for review of the project using the “Ipswich Multi-family Overlay 
District Design Guidelines.”

1. Site Design. 
a. Connections. Sidewalks shall provide a direct connection between building entrances, the 
public sidewalk, bicycle storage, and parking. 
b. Vehicular access. Curb cuts shall be minimized, and shared driveways encouraged. 
c. Open space. Multi-family housing and mixed-use development shall have common outdoor 
space that all residents can access. Such space may be located in any combination of ground 
floor, courtyard, rooftop, or terrace. All outdoor space shall count towards the project’s 
minimum Open Space requirement. 
 
2. Buildings: General. 
a. Position relative to principal street. The primary building shall have its principal façade and 
entrance facing the principal street. See also Section IX.V.G.6. “Buildings: Corner Lots.” 
b. Entries. Entries shall be clearly defined and linked to a paved pedestrian network that includes 
the public sidewalk. 
 
3. Buildings: Multiple buildings on a lot. 
a. For multi-family housing multiple buildings are allowed on the same lot. 
b. For a mixed-use development, uses may be mixed vertically within the buildings or in separate 
buildings. The principal building shall include non-residential uses on the ground floor, as 
required by this Section IX.V.D.1.b. 
c. A paved pedestrian network shall connect parking to the entries to all buildings and the 
buildings to each other, and those buildings adjacent to the public street shall have an entrance 
facing it.  
d. The buildings should be developed in a cohesive manner in which it is obvious that they are 
part of the same development. 
4. Buildings: Mixed-use development. 
a. In a mixed-use building, access to and egress from the residential uses shall be clearly 
differentiated from access to other uses. Such differentiation may occur by using separate 
entrances or egresses from the building or within a lobby space shared among different uses. 
b. Paved pedestrian access from the residential component shall be provided to residential parking 
and amenities and to the public sidewalk, as applicable. 
5. Buildings: Corner Lots. A building on a corner lot shall indicate a primary entrance either along 
one of the street-facing façades or on the primary corner as an entrance serving both streets. 
6. Buildings: Principal Façade and Parking. Parking shall be subordinate in design and location to 
the principal building façade, and whenever possible placed to the rear of the building.
