‭In City Council Dated October _____, 2024‬
‭ORDINANCE‬
‭Be it ordained by the City Council of the City of Brockton to adopt a new section of the Zoning Code as‬
‭follows:‬
‭FORM-BASED CODE DISTRICTS‬
‭1.0 FORM-BASED CODE DISTRICTS‬
‭1.1 Title‬
‭The title of this zone is the Form-Based Code Districts (FBC) and should be added as such to the table in‬
‭Sec. 27-4 – Designation of Zones.‬
‭1.2 Applicability‬
‭The Form-Based Code Districts (FBC) is a zoning district that replaces the underlying districts within the‬
‭boundaries identified in this Ordinance.‬
‭1.3 Purpose‬
‭The purpose of the FBC is to enhance existing mixed-use districts, transit modes, and adjacent‬
‭neighborhoods and accomplish the following goals:‬
‭1.‬ ‭Allow a wide variety of residential building types to meet the City's overall housing goals,‬
‭including the City's strategy to achieve compliance with MGL Chapter 41A, Section 3A (also‬
‭known as the MBTA Communities Act).‬
‭2.‬ ‭Allow commercial and industrial uses that support the City's goals for economic development.‬
‭3.‬ ‭Provide opportunities to support adjacent neighborhoods with retail, services, and jobs.‬
‭4.‬ ‭Create regional destinations that leverage the City's proximity to the commuter rail.‬
‭5.‬ ‭Allow a mix of uses within a single building or on more than one building per lot.‬
‭6.‬ ‭Require certain ground floor uses on specific streets to promote a safe and active pedestrian‬
‭environment.‬
‭7.‬ ‭Incorporate the goals of the Brockton Comprehensive Plan (A Blueprint for Brockton) and local‬
‭neighborhood and Urban Renewal Plans.‬
‭8.‬ ‭Redevelop and enhance the Downtown, our transit station areas, commercial corridors, and‬
‭existing neighborhoods.‬
‭1.4 Severability and Invalidity‬
‭If any provision of this Ordinance or the application of any provision to real property, applicant, or‬
‭property owner is declared unconstitutional or‬‭otherwise‬‭held invalid, the remaining provisions of this‬
‭Ordinance and their application to any other real property, applicant, or property owner remain valid.‬

‭2.0 GLOSSARY AND HOW TO‬
‭2.1 Terms and Definitions‬
‭Unless the context otherwise requires, the following definitions shall be used in the interpretation and‬
‭construction of the Ordinance, and words used in the present tense include the future; the singular number‬
‭shall include the plural, and the plural the singular; the word "building" shall include the word "structure",‬

‭Accessory use‬‭. A use customarily incidental to and‬‭on the same lot or group of lots as a conforming‬
‭principal use.‬
‭Accessory Structure‬‭. Any structure which is incidental‬‭and subordinate to the principal structure, but‬
‭which is located on the same lot as the principal structure, including swimming pools, tennis courts and‬
‭other recreational uses.‬

‭3.0 BROCKTON SUBAREAS‬
‭3.1 Downtown/Trout Brook Subareas‬
‭The purpose of the Downtown/Trout Brook Subarea (DTTB Subarea) is to enhance an existing mixed-use‬
‭district that accomplishes the following goals:‬
‭1.‬ ‭Allow a wide variety of residential building types to meet the City's overall housing goals,‬
‭including the City's strategy to achieve compliance with MGL Chapter 41A, Section 3A (also‬
‭known as the MBTA Communities Act).‬
‭2.‬ ‭Allow commercial and industrial uses that support the City's goals for economic development.‬
‭3.‬ ‭Provide opportunities to support adjacent neighborhoods with retail, services, and jobs.‬
‭4.‬ ‭Create a regional destination that leverages the Downtown's proximity to the commuter rail.‬
‭5.‬ ‭Allow a mix of uses within a single building or on more than one building per lot.‬
‭6.‬ ‭Require certain ground floor uses on specific streets to promote a safe and active pedestrian‬
‭environment.‬
‭7.‬ ‭Incorporate the goals of the Downtown Action Strategy, the Downtown Urban Renewal Plan, and‬
‭the Downtown/Trout Brook Urban Renewal Plan.‬
‭3.1.1 Zoning Districts in the DTTB Subarea‬
‭The Zoning Map identifies the area within which the provisions of the DTTB Subarea apply. The DTTB‬
‭Subarea has twelve districts as shown on the DTTB Subarea Zoning Map. Special considerations for each‬
‭district within this Subarea are defined as follows:‬
‭a.‬ ‭Neighborhood Residential (NR):‬‭None‬
‭b.‬ ‭Urban Residential (UR):‬‭None.‬
‭c.‬ ‭Mixed District (MX-1, MX-2, MX-3):‬‭None.‬
‭d.‬ ‭Commercial Core (CC):‬‭In the DTTB Subarea, the focus‬‭of this district is the area around the‬
‭MBTA Commuter Platform and the Brockton Intermodal Transit Centre to support the‬
‭surrounding MX-3 district.‬
‭e.‬ ‭Commercial Business (CB):‬‭In the DTTB Subarea, the‬‭focus of this district is to support the‬
‭existing grocery store and supporting uses which serve the surrounding neighborhoods.‬
‭f.‬ ‭Commercial Industry (CI):‬‭In the DTTB Subarea, this‬‭district supports job creation and‬
‭retention on the perimeter of the Zone‬
‭g.‬ ‭Civic (CV):‬‭In the DTTB Subarea, this district protects‬‭existing Civic uses which includes‬
‭City Hall and provides guidance for the development or redevelopment of other civic uses.‬

‭a.‬ ‭Primary Streets:‬
‭i.‬
‭In the DTTB Subarea, primary streets are sections of North Main/Main Street, North‬
‭Montello/ Montello Street, Warren Avenue, Commercial St. and Belmont St. These‬
‭streets provide connectivity to the city in general and the towns of Avon (to the north),‬
‭West Bridgewater (to the south), and Easton (to the west).‬
‭ii.‬
‭Active uses are required along 75% of the ground floor façade of a building with‬
‭frontage on Legion Parkway and within the first 30-80 feet of lot depth from the‬
‭principal façade.‬
‭iii.‬
‭Parking‬‭: Curb cuts, surface parking lots and vehicular‬‭entries to structured parking are‬
‭not allowed on Legion Parkway.‬
‭iv.‬
‭Legion Parkway‬‭. This street type is specific to Legion‬‭Parkway, which is the only‬
‭street in the DTTB Subarea with public parking in the center of the street.‬
‭v.‬
‭Setbacks‬‭. The front setback for new construction is‬‭to the lot line, unless the building‬
‭type allows for a public plaza in the front yard setback. A public plaza on Legion‬
‭Parkway shall include outdoor dining, outdoor seating, or other public amenity.‬
‭b.‬ ‭Secondary Streets:‬‭In the DTTB-FBC, the secondary‬‭streets connect the primary streets to‬
‭the rest of the city and may run north/south or east/west.‬
