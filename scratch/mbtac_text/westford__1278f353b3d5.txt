Why are we here?
Chapter 358 of the Acts of 2020 (“Acts”) amended the state zoning act to 
stimulate economic development and address the housing crisis by 
encouraging new housing near transit. It is part of the state’s 2021 Economic 
Development legislation under Governor Baker. Housing development 
continues to be a top priority in Governor Healy’s Administration, as 
evidenced in the $4.1B Affordable Homes Act.

district 
where 
multi-family 
housing is allowed as-of-right, 
meaning without the need for a

waiver, 
or 
other

Why did the state pass this 
law?
Local zoning restrictions on multi-family 
housing  are  contributing to the state’s 
housing crisis. Changing zoning to remove 
these barriers can stimulate the development 
of more diverse types of housing, providing 
places to live for young people, older people 
looking to downsize, and others who cannot 
afford, or do not want, a single-family house.

What does the law mean for Westford?
The Town is required to create a zoning district where multi-family housing is allowed as-of-right.
The zoning district can require Site Plan Review
to
ensure
that
a
project
meets
established
environmental and aesthetic standards, but as long as a project meets the zoning criteria, it must be
approved.
The law requires that the district must:
• Allow for a minimum density of 15 dwelling units per acre
• Be of reasonable size, which for an Adjacent Community, like Westford, means a minimum of 50
acres, at least half of which must be contiguous
• Include land that is feasible to develop
• Allow multi-family housing that is suitable for families with children. For example, it cannot
limit the number of bedrooms in a dwelling unit or the age of residents.
• Create the potential capacity for 924 dwelling units (this number is based on the 2020 Census,
specifically 10% of our year-round housing units). There is no requirement that any of these units
be built, just that our local zoning allow them
The deadline for compliance is December 31, 2024.

What if Westford does not comply?
We become vulnerable to civil enforcement action by the state, and as the Attorney General’s Office 
advised in March 2023, “Communities that fail to comply with the Law’s requirements also risk 
liability under federal and state fair housing laws.” We would also lose access to a variety of state 
funding programs, including MassWorks, the new HousingWorks Infrastructure Program, the 
Local Capital Projects Fund, Community Planning Grants, and Housing Choice, among others. 
Governor Healy also emphasized the importance of compliance, highlighting that the state will 
withhold funding for schools and infrastructure that cities and towns are used to receiving. Opting 
out is not a viable option.
We will also miss out on the opportunity to fulfill a growing need in town for a greater variety of 
housing types and housing affordability.

What is an overlay district?
An Overlay District is a special type of zoning district that is placed over existing zoning 
district(s) or portions thereof to include a set of regulations in addition to the underlying zoning 
district – in this case, for developments that involve the construction of multi-family housing. The 
proposed overlay district does not take away any existing development rights; however, new 
multi-family development within the overlay district would need to adhere to the development 
standards identified in the proposed bylaw.

A Portion of the Nashoba Valley Ski Area
With changing times, increasing energy costs, and climate change, 
it’s been more and more difficult for the Nashoba Valley Ski Area to 
maintain successful winter commercial recreation operations. The 
property owner seeks to expand their commercial recreation uses to 
be more year-round and construct a mixed-use development which 
includes a multi-family housing component. It increases the 
likelihood that the Nashoba Valley Ski Area will continue to be 
used for commercial recreation activities for the very long-term and  
that the businesses at or nearby Nashoba would be more successful, 
having a built-in customer base nearby. Only the areas that 
Nashoba seeks to redevelop for multi-family housing are proposed 
to be included in the district, not the entire property. There are 
opportunities for trail connections in the area.

More area equals more opportunity
Throughout their review process, the MCMAC wrestled with whether they should be 
trying to do the minimum required or whether they should bring forward what they feel 
is the best proposal to Town Meeting, even if the district area would be larger than the 
minimum required to satisfy the mandate. Ultimately the Committee elected to take the 
latter approach, recognizing that more area would provide more opportunity to meet 
several goals and housing needs of current and future residents. The areas that have been 
incorporated into the proposed district will provide short-, medium- and long-term 
redevelopment opportunities that strike a balance between capturing the benefits of 
multi-family development while balancing potential impacts.

Why Should You Consider Supporting This Bylaw?
Diversity of Housing Types
•
Design Guidelines and incentives incorporated into the proposed bylaw can encourage a variety of housing types to be built
•
Design Guidelines also ensure the character and appearance of the development will be attractive and compatible with the 
surrounding area and common housing practices
Affordable and Workforce Housing
•
The MBTA Communities Law allows cities and towns to require that up to 10% of units are affordable, and workforce housing 
may be encouraged
•
Affordable Housing is priced to be affordable to households earning 80% or less of the Area Median Income (AMI)
•
Workforce Housing is priced to be affordable to households earning between 80% and 120% AMI
•
Why is this important? 
•
The state established a goal that each municipality has at least 10% of their housing stock as affordable. Westford is 
currently above that threshold, and this would help ensure we do not drop below 10%
•
There is significant demand for affordable and workforce housing by people of all ages and household types, and this would 
help provide the much-needed housing
•
This helps fulfill goals of the Master Plan and Housing Production Plan

Why Should You Consider 
Supporting This Bylaw?
Pedestrian Improvements
•
Trails – trails may be strongly encouraged within developments, including 
connections to trails on abutting properties.
•
Walkability – developments are intended to be walkable: having sidewalks, 
crosswalks, and other pedestrian infrastructure as well as amenities nearby to 
travel to. This includes connections to existing sidewalk and trail systems.
•
This can also be enhanced by allowing Mixed-use Developments, as 
Commercial components may be allowed within multi-family housing 
developments, such as having ground-floor businesses in a multi-family 
building or in a separate building on the same lot. 
•
Having such amenities nearby is convenient and discourages vehicle use.
Open Space
•
Open space may be required, and additional open space and trails may be 
incentivized. Continuing to protect Westford’s natural resources and provide 
areas for passive recreation is important to this process.
Energy Efficiency
•
All developments in Westford must adhere to the Stretch Code (Building 
Code) which was recently updated to include more energy efficiency 
requirements. 
•
Although Multi-family Housing cannot be held to stricter requirements 
than other uses in town, the proposed bylaw may contain incentives to go 
beyond what is required, such as LEED certification or carbon-neutral 
buildings.

•
By locating the district in areas that have already been disturbed

•

become financially viable for redevelopment
•
Add to the potential customer base for existing businesses, 
particularly retail and restaurant uses

Will the Proposed Bylaw Comply with MBTA 
Communities?
•
The current bylaw language has been submitted to both the Executive Office of Housing and Livable Communities 
and the Attorney General’s Office for a pre-compliance check and is being reviewed by Town Counsel to ensure 
there are no issues prior to Town Meeting
•
To have a compliant district, each community must show that they provide sufficient unit capacity based on the 
State’s Compliance Model
•
The current overlay district area exceeds the minimum requirements per the State’s Compliance Model, with the 
proposed district demonstrating a unit capacity of approximately 5,000 units over approximately 351 acres, 
whereas Westford is required to provide a minimum capacity for the potential of 924 units over approximately 62 
acres
•
However, it is important to point out, the compliance model is not a true build-out analysis as it 
does not factor in certain constraints such as steep slopes or infrastructure limitations such as septic 
system requirements that would pertain to any proposed development
•
Through an iterative process, the MBTA Communities Multi-Family Housing Advisory Committee (MCMAC) 
has taken public feedback and have proposed a district that, though oversized, they feel balances: 
•
Complying with the State Mandate
•
Utilizing the mandate to accomplish several goals and objectives outlined in the Town’s Housing 
Production Plan and Comprehensive Master Plan, and
•
Mitigating the potential impacts of new development by selecting locations that would promote short, 
medium and long-term opportunities as opposed to a large influx of development at any one time
