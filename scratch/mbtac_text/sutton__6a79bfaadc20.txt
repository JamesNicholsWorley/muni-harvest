          2025   Proposed Multifamily Overlay District (MFOD) and Bylaw Fact Sheet&nbsp;  (6/4/25)  August through September 2025  The Planning Board is sponsoring Fall 2025 Town Meeting articles for this district and bylaw as originally drafted by the Housing Production Plan Working Group (HPP) with revisions recommended by Town Counsel.   MBTA Multifamily Overlay District Presentation Slides&nbsp;  (9/24/25)   MBTA Multifamily Overlay District (MMFOD) Overlay District MAP&nbsp;  and  &nbsp;MMFOD BYLAW&nbsp; 9/23/25 post hearing and Select Board commentary version   Public Hearing Notice    Planning Board Recommendation&nbsp;  (September 23, 2025)  The draft bylaw and district were submitted to the State Executive Office of Housing and Livable Communities (EOHLC) in May for pre adoption review. After the State came back with their commentary, Town Counsel reviewed the proposed bylaws with this commentary in mind and the  &nbsp;HPP Working Group Draft Bylaws&nbsp;  were revised in consult with the Chair of the HPP and the Planning Board in advance of the Planning Board public hearing. Sutton voters must approve a compliant bylaw and overlay district by December 31, 2025 to remain in compliance with the law.  Approximately 140 of 177 MBTA communities have passed bylaws/districts to comply with the law, although 35 are awaiting final review by the Executive Office of Housing and Livable Communities(EOHLC) and the Attorney General.  July 31, 2025  &nbsp;Compliance Status by Town     January 8, 2025 Attorney General v. Town of Milton Decided    January 15, 2025 Emergency Regulations Issued   January 31, 2025 to February 21, 2025 11:59 pm - Public Comment Period  &nbsp;Mass.gov    2/7/25 Sutton Planning Board letter to State    2/21/25 State Auditor opinion regarding 40A Section 3a status as unfunded mandate (Methuen)    January - June 2025  The Housing Production Plan working group drafted and recommended the adoption of an MBTA District that overlays the Market 32, Mickey&apos;s Car Wash and Convenient MD Parcels at 10 and 17 Galaxy Pass and 15 Pleasant Valley Road ot the Planning Board. You can get more  details&nbsp; on the HPP Working Group &nbsp; .  2024  A postcard was sent to every household in Sutton and distributed to employers in Sutton with a survey link/information on how to get a hard copy survey and advertising three public education and input sessions. The survey and sessions focused on Sutton demographics and housing statistics and seeking input related to housing in general as well as both Chapter 40A Section 3a Multifamily Housing Law (2021) and Chapter 40B Affordable housing Law (1969).    5/30/24 Postcard&nbsp;  &nbsp;Survey&nbsp;  &nbsp;Survey Results    6/24 Home in Sutton PowerPoint Presentation    6/20/24 Home in Sutton Discussion Video    2023  In January 2023, the Town of Sutton submitted an Action Plan including a map of potential housing sites in order to remain in compliance with the provisions of C. 40A Section 3A.    January 30, 2023 Action Plan Filing    January 30, 2023 Map of Potential Housing Sites    8/2023 Revised Guidelines Issued to address mixed use structures    In accordance with the Town&apos;s Action Plan, the Town sought a Massachusetts Housing Partnership Grant for consulting assistance to help compile housing and demographic information and undertake education and public outreach/input for Sutton&apos;s potential multi family housing bylaw. In October 2023 Sutton was awarded the grant and began working with Central Massachusetts Regional Planning Commission (CMRPC) starting in early 2024.  2022  In early 2022, Draft Guidelines for compliance with the law were released and the Town provided commentary relative to concerns with the proposed guidelines.    January 5, 2022 Draft Guidelines    2/17/22 Planning Director&apos;s Memo    3/1/22 Presentation to Select Board    3/7/22 Sutton&apos;s Comments to the State    8/10/22 Final Guidelines Issued    8/17/22 Planning Director&apos;s Memo    2021  In early 2021 Governor Baker signed into law an economic development bill. As economic development is dependent on the availability of workers and in some cases consumers, the bill included provisions intended to increase multifamily housing stock, especially in areas with access to transit.
