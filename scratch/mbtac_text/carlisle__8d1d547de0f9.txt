feasibility of compliance with MBTA Communities legislation and guidelines, which is a strategy identified 
within the Housing Production Plan. This effort will dovetail and thus be combined with Master Plan 
Recommendation 19a: Consider adopting an additional or alternative Open Space Residential Development 
or Conservation Cluster bylaw.

Background Information 
What is MBTA Communities? 
M.G.L. Chapter 40A Section 3A, commonly known as “MBTA Communities” legislation intends to address 
the housing crisis in Massachusetts by requiring communities served by - or in the case of Carlisle, adjacent 
to communities that are served by - the MBTA, to zone for additional housing (a percentage based on the

Why & how does it apply to Carlisle? 
Great question! According to the Guidelines promulgated by the Department of Housing and Community 
Development (DHCD), most recently revised October 20221, Carlisle is classified as an “Adjacent Small 
Town” which derives from our classification as an “Other Served Community” pursuant to M.G.L. Chapter 
161A Section 1.  
 
As an “Adjacent Small Town” Carlisle is required to zone for the following: 
• Minimum Capacity: 5% of the Town’s 2020 housing unit count (1,897 units) = 95 units 
• Minimum Gross Density: 15 units/acre, with the following considerations (p10-11 of Guidelines): 
o Gross Density is defined as “a units-per-acre density measurement that includes land occupied by 
public rights-of-way and any recreational, civic, commercial and other non-recreational uses.” The 
Gross Density calculation also includes private-rights-of-way. 
o Gross Density calculation excludes the following lands: surface waterbodies, wetlands & buffers, 
protected open space, and land needed to protect private/public water supplies. 
o Water & Wastewater Infrastructure: installation of new infrastructure or expanding capacity of 
existing infrastructure is not required for compliance. 
o “In most cases, multi-family housing can be created using private septic and wastewater treatment 
systems that meet state environmental standards.” 
o “While the zoning must allow for gross average density of at least 15 units per acre, there may be 
other legal or practical limitations, including lack of infrastructure or infrastructure capacity, that 
result in actual housing production at lower density than the zoning allows.” 
o “…even though the zoning district must permit 15 units per acre as of right, any multi-family 
housing produced within the district is subject to, and must comply with, the state wetlands 
protection act and title 5 of the state environmental code – even if such compliance means a 
proposed project will be less dense than 15 units per acre.” 
• Minimum Land Area: Not applicable 
• % Within 1/2 mile of MBTA: Not applicable 
• As-of-Right: We cannot require a special permit, variance, or other discretionary permit, but we can 
have projects utilizing this zoning be subject to Site Plan Review by the Planning Board. In addition, 
said projects would still be subject to review by the Board of Health and Conservation Commission, 
and would go through the same internal review process as other development projects in Town. 
Are there penalties for non-compliance? 
The short answer is yes. Communities that fail to comply with the legislation will not be eligible for the 
following funding: (1) Housing Choice grants [Carlisle does not qualify for this anyway]; (2) Local Capital 
Projects Fund established via the Resort Casino legislation [loss of up to 10% of local housing authority 
budget, so not applicable to Carlisle as I understand it]; (3) MassWorks infrastructure grants.
