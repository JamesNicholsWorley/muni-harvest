         Long Term Zoning  Chapter 3A does allow for a planning strategy which could lessen the immediate and/or long-term effect of adding 750 units and it is referred under paragraph section 5, d;  &ldquo; &hellip;Demonstrating compliance with the minimum multi-family unit capacity requires only that an MBTA community show that the zoning allows multi-family housing as-of-right and that a sufficient number of multi-family housing units could be added to or replace existing uses and structures over time &ndash; even though such additions or replacements may be unlikely to occur.&rdquo;

         FAQs   When did the law go into effect?   In 2020, Governor Baker signed into law Section 3A of the Zoning Act, which states that Weston, along with 177 other communities Massachusetts where MBTA stations/stops are located or with access to MBTA service, shall create a district(s) where multifamily (3 or more units within a building )housing is allowed &lsquo;as-of-right&rsquo;. The &lsquo;Final Guidelines&rsquo; for the law were posted by the Executive Office of Housing and Livable Communities (EOHLC), in August 2022, with revisions in October 2022, and August 2023.&nbsp;   What is the purpose of 3A?   Section 3A seeks to help alleviate the housing crisis in the Commonwealth through &lsquo;by-right&rsquo; zoning reform by significantly increasing every town&rsquo;s multifamily inventory . 3A requires that a certain percentage of the mandated units be located within a half mile radius (considered a walkable distance) of a train stop in order in an attempt facilitate the use of public transportation thereby minimizing an increase to another major problem facing the Commonwealth &ndash; traffic.&nbsp;   What specific requirements must Weston meet?    We are required to create zoning district(s) that collectively contain a minimum of 50 acres with a minimum zoned capacity of 750 units. There is no requirement that the Town or anyone build the units, just that the zoning be created to allow them by right.  The units per acre density&nbsp;across the zoning district(s)&nbsp;must be a minimum of 15 units per acre.  40% of the 750 units (300 units) and 40% of the 50 acres (20 acres) need to be located with &frac12; mile of the station.&nbsp;  Subdistricts are allowed and but must be a minimum of 5 acres. &nbsp;One of the subdistricts must be at least 50% of the total district which is 25 acres.  We are required to adopt a District by 31 December 2024 to remain compliant with state law, so w need to approve the rezoning by-law and district locations at the Fall Town meeting. Normally to approve a by-law change through town meeting, there needs to be a 2/3 majority. Chapter 3A changed this to a simple majority to count as approval    What does &lsquo;by-right&rsquo; or &lsquo;as-of-right&rsquo; mean?   &nbsp; &nbsp;    Any zoning use which is labeled &lsquo;as-of-right&rsquo; or &lsquo;by-right&rsquo; within a zoning district, sub-district or overlay district means that that a project which has that use, needs no special or discretionary zoning review or approval. A site plan review by the Planning Board may be allowed if stated in the by-law. FYI: Most residential properties in Weston are in zone A,B,C or D meaning that a single-family home is the only by-right or as-of-right use. &nbsp;   What can the Bylaw regulate?   The bylaw can contain dimensional and bulk requirements typically contained in a zoning bylaw such as building height, setbacks, parking, lighting, noise, signage, traffic access, mechanical and trash location and screening, plantings, and impervious surface..&nbsp;   What can the Bylaw not regulate?   The bylaw cannot regulate age, architectural style, number of bedrooms, impose more restrictive building or energy codes, impose higher taxes than currently exist in town,&nbsp;   What is a Site Plan Review by the Planning Board?   Although it has less discretionary power than a Special Permit Review, a Site Plan Review does give the Planning Board power over the following aspects of the development of a property. Specifically, this includes;   Site sensitivity  Preserve natural and historic features  Maximize open space  Minimize site disturbance  Screen objectionable features  Adequate water, sewer, erosion, storm water management  Minimize town services  Design harmony  Underground utilities  Setback of objectional features  Minimize shadows,  Limit glare   Historically, the Planning Board has been thorough, strict and diligent in the review of all these aspects   Can we re-zone areas which already have buildings?   Yes, whether they are single family homes or commercial office buildings/laboratories, or retail. &nbsp;The re-zoning would keep the current as-of-right use, but add multi-family housing as another as-of-right use.   Which state grants would Weston be denied if we don&rsquo;t comply with 3A?   As of 3-20-24, the program funding we would be denied are:   Community Planning Grants  Massachusetts Downtown Initiative  Urban Agenda  Rural and Small Town Development Fund  Brownfields Redevelopment Fund  Site Readiness Program  Underutilized Properties Program  Collaborative Workspace Program  Real Estate Services Technical Assistance  Commonwealth Places Programs  Land Use Planning Grants  Local Acquisitions for Natural Diversity  Municipal Vulnerability Preparedness (MVP) Planning and Project Grants    Does Chapter 3A require any of the housing units to qualify as affordable under the Subsidized Housing Inventory (SHI)?   No. However, Chapter 3A does allow each town to require that 10% of the units be affordable. In this way the additional 750 units won&rsquo;t affect the current SHI percentage. The state will allow a greater affordability requirement if the Town can provide a feasibility study showing that development would still be feasible with the affordability requirements.&nbsp;   Is there Multifamily housing currently in Weston?
