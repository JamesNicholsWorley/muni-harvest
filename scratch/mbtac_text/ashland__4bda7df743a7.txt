MBTA Communities - Frequently Asked Questions 
 
1. What is the MBTA Communities Act? 
• 
The MBTA Communities Act was passed in January 2021 and requires communities that 
host an MBTA station, host a T stop, or are “adjacent communities”, to zone for 
additional multi-family housing by right based on the community’s Housing Stock.  
 
2. What towns and cities do this law apply to?  
• 
The MBTA Communities Act applies to communities that host an MBTA commuter rail 
station, a T stop, or are considered “adjacent communities” or “adjacent small towns.”   
• 
Each designation has different requirements for compliance.  Ashland is a “Commuter 
Rail Community.”

3. What are the basic Multi-Family Zoning Requirements for MBTA Communities for Ashland? 
• 
Housing Units:  As a Commuter Rail Community, Ashland has to zone for a number of 
dwelling units equal to 15% of its existing housing units, or 1,124 units.  This does not 
mean that the units need to be built. 
• 
Land Area:  The MBTA zoned land area has to be a minimum of 50 acres. 
• 
Density:  Zoned areas must have a minimum density of 15 units per acre.  
 
4. Are there any maximum density requirements? 
• 
There is no required maximum density, but the State’s zoning model takes building 
height, setbacks and parking into consideration.  The site must be able to actually 
accommodate the proposed density. 
 
5. If we adopt the recommended configuration in Ashland, what areas will be impacted? 
• 
The proposed bylaw has 5 very specific sub-districts where MBTA multi-family housing 
could be located.  The MBTA zoning would only pertain to these specific areas, which 
total approximately 56 acres of Ashland’s 7,936 land acres. 
 
6. What’s the difference between the 40B law and MBTA Communities requirements? 
• 
Chapter 40B was enacted in 1969 to help address the shortage of affordable housing.  It 
allows developers that pursue the 40B process to bypass local zoning requirements in 
exchange for providing 25% of the total units at affordable prices.  Chapter 40B projects 
are allowed when a community does not have at least 10% of its total housing units set 
aside as affordable.   
• 
The MBTA Communities Act was signed into law in 2021 and focuses on zoning, not 
individual development projects.  MBTA Communities requires communities with or 
adjacent to commuter rail stations or T stops to zone for multi-family housing near 
MBTA stations.  See the attached chart for additional information.

7. Can MBTA Zoning include land that is already built on? 
• 
Yes, zoning only considers the land beneath a site, not what is currently built (or not 
built) on it.  MBTA zoning can include empty parcels or ones that already have buildings 
on them.  
 
8. What happens if Ashland does not adopt MBTA Communities requirements? 
• 
If Ashland does not comply with the guidelines of MBTA Communities, Ashland would 
be ineligible for certain state housing, infrastructure, and transportation funding 
programs. Non-compliant communities could also be liable for litigation. There is 
currently litigation between the Commonwealth and the Town of Milton, which has 
opted not to comply.  A precedent decision will likely be issued by the Massachusetts 
Supreme Judicial Court (SJC) in the upcoming weeks.

MBTA Communities Zoning is Different than 40B Projects 
 
 
In summary, MBTA zoning is not 40B.  40B is a tool that allows a developer to override local bylaws with permitting through 
the Zoning Board of Appeals when a municipality has not met the required affordable housing production. The MBTA zoning 
law requires the creation of zoning for multi-family housing near transit in communities served by the MBTA, regardless of 
their existing affordable housing stock levels, upon review by the Planning Board. MBTA Communities does not require a 
community to build any housing, only zone for it.
