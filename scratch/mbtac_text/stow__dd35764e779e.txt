Does Stow need to comply 
with the “MBTA Communities 
Law”? 
Yes, because...
 
• This law is not an “opt-in” program 
of the state. However, Stow can 
create zoning that maximizes the 
benefits to the Town, such as the 
location or type of multifamily 
housing allowed. 
  
• The State Supreme Court has 
determined 
that 
the 
law 
is 
constitutional and that all subject 
communities must comply. 
   
• The Supreme Court confirmed 
that the Attorney General has the 
authority to enforce the law. 
   
• Stow will lose eligibility for state 
grants that keep our community 
safe and functioning. 
 
• In addition to losing eligibility for 
grants, Stow will be subject to 
heavy legal fees if sued by the 
Attorney General. 
   
• The Town of Milton, the first non- 
complaint MBTA Community, has 
expended almost $300,000 in 
legal fees; Milton is now seeking 
compliance to avoid additional 
costs. 
To learn more:  
Visit the Planning Board’s website at
www.Stow-MA.gov/Planning-Board/
pages/MBTA-Communities
