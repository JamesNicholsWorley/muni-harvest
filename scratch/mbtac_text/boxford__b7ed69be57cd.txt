          MBTA Communities Law &amp; December 17 Special Town Meeting Information    Special Town Meeting &ndash; Wednesday, December 17, 2025 &bull; 7:00 PM    Masconomet High School Auditorium        Your Vote Determines Compliance &amp; Local Control    Massachusetts General Laws Chapter 40A &sect;3A, known as the MBTA Communities Law, requires all MBTA-designated communities&mdash;including Boxford&mdash;to adopt at least one zoning district that allows multifamily housing by right. This law is  mandatory  and  not optional . The Supreme Judicial Court has confirmed that the law is constitutional, enforceable, and cannot be disregarded or opted-out by municipalities.   Link to summary information sheet.    Link to Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ) sheet.    Warrant Article 8: MBTA Communities Overlay District   At the December 17 Special Town Meeting, voters will decide whether to adopt a compliant MBTA Communities Multifamily Overlay District. Adoption of the district:   Retains Boxford&rsquo;s control over zoning  Avoids state-imposed zoning  Protects 2-acre rural zoning across the community  Maintains eligibility for significant state grant funds  Prevents legal enforcement action by the Attorney General    Grants at Risk    $1.2 million currently awarded  More than $5 million in grant eligibility over the coming five years       What the Proposed Overlay Does   The Select Board has developed an overlay district that:   Meets state compliance standards  Minimizes realistic development potential  Preserves Boxford&rsquo;s rural character, including existing 2-acre zoning  Uses a legally recognized &ldquo;paper compliance&rdquo; method   The overlay creates a compliant zoning pathway but does  not  authorize construction or redevelopment.      Location: The Willows at Boxford   The proposed overlay district is located at The Willows at Boxford. &nbsp;  Link to proposed&nbsp;   district map   &nbsp;and zoning&nbsp;   bylaw amendment   . &nbsp; The Willows is a condominium community where redevelopment is highly unlikely due to:   Private condominium ownership structure  Required owner approval to dissolve condominium status &nbsp;(75% of all unit owners must vote to remove condominium status before any redevelopment could occur)  Existing full buildout and deed restrictions  Financial impracticality of demolition and reconstruction    Key Clarification    The overlay district satisfies state zoning requirements but does not enable multifamily construction without substantial condominium owner support. No redevelopment can proceed without owner vote and legal restructuring.      Why Compliance Matters    This is State Law   Compliance is required regardless of local preference. Not adopting the overlay does  not&nbsp; exempt Boxford from the law.   If the district is not adopted:    The state may impose a zoning district in a location not chosen by Boxford  A state-imposed district would prioritize unit capacity and development feasibility&mdash;not rural preservation  The Attorney General may pursue legal enforcement  Boxford becomes ineligible for key infrastructure, education, and resiliency grants       State Pre-Adoption Review   Boxford submitted the draft overlay district to the Executive Office of Housing and Livable Communities (EOHLC) for review prior to Town Meeting.   State planning staff identified no compliance-blocking issues  EOHLC indicated that adoption of the Willows overlay &ldquo;would put the Town in a good position for compliance if adopted.&rdquo;   Formal certification can only occur after Town Meeting adoption, per state process.      Paper Compliance Approach   Many Massachusetts communities have adopted zoning districts that technically satisfy the MBTA Communities Law while limiting actual redevelopment potential. The state recognizes this strategy as long as zoning:   Is mapped in an eligible location  Meets the required acreage and capacity formula  Allows multifamily housing by right  on paper    This is the approach Boxford is employing to meet statutory requirements and protect local land use standards.      Local Control Through Compliance   By adopting the proposed overlay, Boxford maintains authority over:   District siting  Dimensional design standards  Long-term zoning impact management   If the community does not adopt, the state&mdash;not Boxford&mdash;selects the location and parameters for multifamily zoning.      Financial Impact at a Glance        Funding Category      Estimated Impact         Grants currently at risk     $1.2 million       Future eligible grants over five years     Approx. $5 million       Potential projects impacted    Rails to Trails, bridge &amp; culvert work, resilience planning, literacy funding, infrastructure          December 17th Special Town Meeting Participation    December 17, 2025 at 7:00 PM  Masconomet High School Auditorium     This decision rests entirely with the voters of Boxford. The Select Board has completed planning and compliance design; approval of zoning can only occur through Town Meeting vote.     What is the MBTA Communities Law?  The MBTA Communities Law, passed by the State Legislature in January 2021, included a new  Section 3A  of Massachusetts zoning statute, Chapter 40A. &nbsp;Section 3A requires an MBTA Community to have a zoning by-law that provides for at least 1 district in which multi-family housing is permitted by right. &nbsp;Furthermore, the district shall have a minimum gross density of 15 units per acre. &nbsp;Per State designation, the Town of Boxford is considered a MBTA Community. &nbsp;Furthermore, Boxford, as a rural town adjacent to Haverhill, a community with commuter rail service, is required by section 3A to establish a district with a capacity for 141 housing units.  More information on the MBTA Communities requirements can be found  on the state website . &nbsp;It should be emphasized that construction of housing units is not a requirement of the MBTA regulations. &nbsp;Only the zoning is required.  Although the Town has an approved MBTA Action Plan, the Town has made clear to the state that compliance will be particularly difficult for a community like Boxford which is rural in character. &nbsp;The density requirements in Section 3A would be a dramatic change from local land use patterns. It will be a challenge for the Town of Boxford to create a zoning by-law in compliance with Section 3A, particularly when considering the limits and constraints in terms of limited infrastructure and services, reliance on septic and well systems, and the abundance of wetlands and conservation lands.  As a designated Small Adjacent Community, the Town of Boxford has a deadline of December 31, 2025 to adopt a zoning district that complies with the MBTA Communities legislation.  Public Information Session October 20, 2025  The Boxford Select Board and Planning Board held a joint public information session on October 20th, which included a presentation on the MTBA Communities Law and the town&apos;s efforts to date. &nbsp;Below are links to the slideshow and a recording of the information session:    Presentation, October 20th Public Information Session    Recording, October 20th Public Information Session        Select Board Refers Willows Overlay District for Public Hearing   At its October 6, 2025 meeting, the Boxford Select Board voted to refer a proposed MBTA Communities (3A) compliant overlay zoning district at the Willows at Boxford to the Planning Board for a public hearing. This proposed district is the Select Board&rsquo;s preferred location to bring forward to voters at the December 17, 2025 Special Town Meeting.   Link to proposed&nbsp;   district map   &nbsp;and zoning&nbsp;   bylaw amendment   .   The decision follows the state&rsquo;s pre-adoption review, which confirmed that the Willows overlay district meets the technical requirements of the MBTA Communities Law (G.L. c. 40A, &sect;3A). Adoption of this district would keep Boxford in compliance with the law and maintain the Town&rsquo;s eligibility for important state funding programs.  The Select Board emphasized that the Willows proposal represents a &ldquo;paper compliance&rdquo; approach&mdash;meaning it fulfills the state&rsquo;s requirements without creating a realistic potential for new, multi-family, high density housing development. The Willows is an existing condominium community that is already built out. As confirmed by Town Counsel, under both Massachusetts condominium law (G.L. c. 183A &sect;19) and the Willows condominium trust documents, removing land from the condominium or redeveloping it would require approval by at least 75% of unit owners and all lien holders. This makes any future redevelopment effectively impossible.  Given these restrictions, the Select Board determined that designating the Willows as the Town&rsquo;s MBTA Communities district is the most prudent and responsible path forward. The district allows Boxford to remain in compliance with state law while making it highly unlikely that no new development would occur as a result of the zoning change.  The Planning Board will hold a public hearing on   November 12, 2025 ,  and residents will have the opportunity to vote on the proposed overlay at the   December 17, 2025 Special Town Meeting  .
