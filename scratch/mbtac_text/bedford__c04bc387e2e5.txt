Bedford Housing Partnership Urge Support for Proposed Bylaw to     
Comply with State MBTA Communities Act   
The Bedford Housing Partnership (Housing Partnership | Bedford, MA (bedfordma.gov), at its meeting in 
January 16, 2024,  urged support for the proposed bylaw that will be presented to voters at  the 
Bedford Town Meeting on March 25th.  Passage of the bylaw is needed to comply with the new 
Massachusetts MBTA Communities Act known generally as the Multifamily Zoning Law.  
This law requires that each of the 177 MBTA communities in Eastern Massachusetts designate an 
area or areas in their community where multi-family housing is allowed by-right. The Town must 
designate at least 50 acres where multi-family housing is allowed by-right and allow a housing density 
of 15 units per acre in the designated area.  There cannot be age or other restrictions that would 
make units unsuitable for families with children. Failure to comply with this law would lead to severe 
financial consequences including the loss of future state grant opportunities as well as possibly being 
sued by the Massachusetts Office of the Attorney General.  
Led by the Bedford Planning Board, the Town is working diligently to come into compliance with the 
new law.  This process included a number a public meetings, surveys and public hearings.  The 
Planning Board is now finishing the selection of two housing overlay districts. One centers on the 
Depot Park/Railroad Ave area continuing down Loomis Street to a section of Great Road. The second 
overlay district called Shawsheen West covers the commercial area of Alfred Circle and Roberts Drive 
to Great Road. Together the two districts total 55 acres and have a unit capacity of 759 units.  
Why  
There has been a shortage of housing construction for the last many decades in the Commonwealth 
which is a major reason housing costs keep increasing. The problem compounds itself which is why 
the median prices for a standard single-family home has increased in Bedford from $375,000 in 2000 
to $949,950 in 2022 or 153% and from $698,000 in 2017 or an increase of 36% in just 5 years.1 
The fact remains that Eastern Massachusetts of which Bedford is a part, has been and remains in a 
housing crisis.  Not only are housing of all types becoming more expensive, there is not enough 
housing of all types being built, particularly affordable housing. 
• 31% of Bedford renters are cost-burdened meaning they are paying more than 30% of their income 
toward rent.2

• Fewer than 50% of household can afford the median rent for 2 or 3BR units in 2022 without being 
cost burdened. In 2023 an income of $142,800 is needed to afford the median 2BR rent of $3,570 a 
month and $160,000 is needed to afford the median 3BR rent of $4,000 a month.3  
• The burden is even greater on Seniors. 30% of households headed by someone 65+ have an income 
of $50,000 or less while only 8% of households headed by someone under 65 years of age have an 
income of $50,000 or less.4 
• Buying a house is too expensive for many current residents.  Median sales price of a house in 2022 is 
$949,950.5  A household would need an income of $248,306 to afford this.  Yet the median income in 
Bedford is $140,467 which affords a home of $504,700.6 
• Massachusetts ranks 42nd out of 50 in the number of housing permits approved per capita. This is half 
of the average housing permit rate.7 
Benefits 
There are a number of benefits that will come from complying with this law allowing multi-family 
housing by-right. These include:  
• Provide people who are employed in Bedford or who grew up in Bedford greater opportunities to live 
in town including our public-school teachers and municipal workers.  Greater densities and smaller 
units are expected to be lower in cost. 
• Provide downsizing options for seniors. 
• Support first time homebuyers and young families. 
• Provide additional affordable housing opportunities, and broader missing middle housing designs. 
Ultimately, the goal is to provide adequate housing that is inclusive of all household types.  
The Bedford Housing Partnership urges the public to participate fully in this process and support 
compliance with the new law.  The time to discuss and provide input on options is now.
