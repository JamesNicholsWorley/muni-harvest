MBTA Communities Multifamily Zoning		 
	
		 
			

		   				
	 
	
	 	
 
	     


          UPDATE    The Beverly City Council adopted the Zoning changes associated with compliance with the MBTA Communities Act on November 12, 2024. Subsequently, the Planning Department submitted the compliance application to the state by December 31, 2024, as required. Beverly&apos;s Section 3A Zoning Compliance was confirmed in April 2025.&nbsp;  
     

 
 
	   

	   

			      
		   				
	 
	
	 	
 
	     


         The  MBTA Communities Law  (adopted in 2021 and codified in MGL Chapter 40A, Section 3A) established a multifamily zoning requirement for the 177 Massachusetts communities in the MBTA service district. While the type of transit may vary in these municipalities, all are in close enough proximity that they benefit from nearby transit amenities.  The City of Beverly is in the  Commuter Rail&nbsp; category for the MBTA Communities Law. &nbsp;This means that Beverly needs to have a District that allows multifamily housing by-right (not requiring a discretionary approval process) that is at least 50 acres in size, within 1/2 mile of one of the train stations, and has a minimum gross density of   15 housing units per acre. &nbsp;  This district is required to contain 15% of of the total of of the 2020 census housing unit count for the entire city as multifamily units:  17,887 total housing units x 0.15 = 2,683 multifamily housing units within the proposed district required to comply    
     

 
 
	   

	   

			    				
	 
	
	 	

 
    
	  	  
	   

	   
	 
	
	 	
 
	     


          &nbsp; &nbsp; &nbsp; &nbsp; &nbsp; &nbsp; &nbsp;The City of Beverly is classified as a Commuter Rail Community

         City of Beverly Proposed MBTA Communities Overlay District 
     

 
 
	   

	   
	 
	
	 	

 
    
	  	  
	   

	   

			      
		   				
	 
	
	 	
 
	     


          In a few cases, some of the properties shown on the map above have &quot;split&quot; zoning, meaning that the zoning line between two zones splits the property. &nbsp;For those properties, the zone that encompasses the majority of the property is shown. &nbsp;This map is for MBTA Communities Law compliance only and is not an official City of Beverly Zoning Map.  
     

 
 
	   

	   

			      
		   				
	 
	
	 	
 
	     


         Proposed MBTA Communities Overlay District Zoning     The City of Beverly already has Zoning around the Beverly Depot Commuter Rail Station that allows multifamily housing by right. &nbsp;In order to comply with the required Zoning Capacity of 2,683 housing units within this area, some changes to the Zoning will need to be made. &nbsp;This is due largely because the vast majority (approximately 80%) of the existing parcels in the RHD and RMD zones where multifamily housing is allowed by right are non-conforming to the current Zoning. &nbsp;This means that some aspect of these parcels does not comply with the City&apos;s current Zoning Ordinance. &nbsp;Some examples, including but not limited to, of non-conformity could be that the parcels are smaller than the required minimum lot size or the existing building on the parcel is within one of the required yard setbacks.&nbsp; 
     

 
 
	   

	   

			    				
	 
	
	 	
 
	     


         MMU - Mandatory Mixed Use  CC - Central Business  CC2 - Central Business Cabot  RHD - Multifamily High Density  RMD - Multifamily Medium Density    
     

 
 
	   

	   

			      
		   				
	 
	
	 	
 
	     


         Other changes in order to achieve compliance have to do with minimum lot size requirements for buildings that are residential  only&nbsp; in the CC/CC2 Zoning Districts. Currently a building that has a residential component in these zones requires a minimum lot size of 1,000 square feet per dwelling unit. &nbsp;A building that has a commercial component added to it along with the residential component (mixed-use), however, does not have  any  minimum lot size requirements. &nbsp;The minimum lot size requirements for an all residential building will be revised to match the mixed-use minimum lot size requirements.&nbsp;    These zoning changes will be made by creating an MBTA Communities Overlay District and will only apply to parcels that are zoned RHD, RMD, CC, or CC2 in the MBTA Communities Overlay District as shown on the above map.   &nbsp;  The MMU (Mandatory Mixed Use) is a separate proposed Overlay District that coincides with the majority of the existing Depot Parking Overlay District. &nbsp;By including the MMU, the required Zoning Capacity in order to comply with MBTA Communities Law can be reduced by 25% and is explained in much greater detail below.

            Mandatory Mixed Use Overlay District (MMU)     On August 17, 2023, The MBTA Communities Law was modified to offer MBTA Communities a path to receive some credit for mixed-use development districts called  Mandatory Mixed Use (pdf) :   Allow an MBTA Community to &quot;offset&quot; the minimum multifamily housing unit capacity requirement in certain multifamily zoning district(s) by up to 25%, based on the unit capacity of a mixed-use zoning district that meets key requirements of the MBTA Communities Law, except the multifamily zoning district  requires  a ground floor non-residential component.  In order for a MMU District to be eligible to offset the required unit capacity, the district must meet the following standards:  The MMU District is in an eligible location where existing village-style or downtown development is essential to preserve pedestrian access to amenities.  The&nbsp;  Massachusetts Executive Office of Housing and Livable Communities (EOHLC)  &nbsp;pre-approved the location of the MMU on May 20, 2024.   There are no age restrictions or limits on unit size, number of bedrooms, bedroom size, or number of occupants and the residential units permitted are suitable for children.  Mixed-use development in the district is allowed &quot;as of right&quot;.  The requirement for non-residential uses is limited to the ground floor of buildings, and in no case represents a requirement that more than 33% of the floor area of a building, lot, or project must be for non-residential uses.  The requirement for non-residential uses does not preclude a minimum of three residential dwelling units per lot.  The requirement for non-residential uses allows a broad mix of non-residential uses &quot;as of right&quot; in keeping with the nature of the area   There are no minimum parking requirements associated with the non-residential uses allowed as of right.      The area that was chosen for the MMU District is the area along Rantoul Street roughly from Bow Street south to Edwards Street and is  mostly&nbsp; within the existing Depot Parking Overlay District. 
     

 
 
	   

	   

			    				
	 
	
	 	

 
    
	  	  
	   

	   
	 
	
	 	
 
	     


          Proposed MMU District (red) with existing Depot Parking Overlay District (blue)  
     

 
 
	   

	   

			      
		   				
	 
	
	 	
 
	     


         The Depot Parking Overlay District was established in 2011 after determining that a reduction in the parking requirement for two-bedroom dwelling units within walking distance of the Beverly MBTA Depot is reflective of actual demand and would be beneficial in encouraging &quot;smart growth&quot; residential development. &nbsp;The Depot Parking Overlay District is shown on the official City of Beverly Zoning Map. &nbsp;The off-street parking requirement for one and two bedroom dwelling units located in the Depot Parking Overlay District is one off-street parking space per dwelling unit. &nbsp;The addition of the MMU would require that   there are no parking minimums for commercial uses within the MMU.  &nbsp; Language therefore needs to be added to the Zoning Ordinance removing parking requirements for commercial uses in the Depot Parking Overlay District.  The main benefit of establishing a MMU District is to be able reduce the required number of multifamily housing units in the proposed MBTA Communities District by a maximum of 25% based on the multifamily housing capacity of the MMU. &nbsp; According to the proposed Zoning changes in the summary above for the CC Zoning of the properties within the proposed MMU District, the MMU has a multifamily housing unit capacity well above the maximum allowable 25% offset. &nbsp;As stated before, the City of Beverly is required to have a District that is, among other criteria, capable of containing 2,683 multifamily housing units and by establishing a MMU District, that number can be reduced to:  2,683 required multifamily housing units x 25% MMU offset = total reduction of 670 multifamily housing units  2,683 required multifamily housing units - 670 MMU multifamily housing units =   2,013 required multifamily housing units   
     

 
 
	   

	   

			      
		   				
	 
	
	 	
 
	     


         MBTA Communities Law Compliance Model Results     Using the MBTA Communities Compliance Model provided by the EOHLC and inputting the following information:   The MMU District Capacity offset  The Zoning dimensional requirements (building height, lot size, setbacks, parking requirements, etc.)&nbsp;  The current zoning dimensional requirements for the RHD and RMD zones and the removal of the lot size square footage increases based on the number of dwelling units in a building on a lot and replacing them with a unit cap  the current zoning dimensional requirements for the CC/CC2 zones and the removal of the 1,000 square foot per residential unit lot size requirements     The MBTA Communities Law compliance model shows that the City of Beverly&apos;s Proposed MBTA Communities District complies with the MBTA Communities Law       Proposed MBTA Communities District compliance highlights:    Multifamily housing unit capacity of 2,063 multifamily housing units (2,013 required)    Approximately 21 housing units per acre (15 required)    District size is approximately 98 acres (50 required)    Land area and housing unit capacity are almost entirely within 1/2 mile of the Beverly Depot train station&nbsp;   
     

 
 
	   

	   

			      
		   				
	 
	
	 	
 
	     


           Compliance does NOT mean that 2,063 multifamily housing units will be built! &nbsp;      It means that the zoning for the district has the&nbsp;    capacity    &nbsp;for 2,063 multifamily housing units   
     

 
 
	   

	   

			      
		   				
	 
	
	 	
 
	     


          A common misconception with MBTA Communities Law is that it requires multifamily housing to be built. &nbsp;The law requires that exactly  zero&nbsp; multifamily housing units be built. &nbsp;What the law requires is that a district exists, with the criteria previously mentioned, where multifamily housing  could&nbsp; be built.   The City of Beverly already has zoning districts where multifamily housing can be built by right. &nbsp;As a result, the adoption of the proposed MBTA Communities District will likely have little impact on the City. &nbsp;No use changes are proposed in any of the Zoning Districts that are within the proposed MBTA Communities Overlay District so what could be built on a property within the District will remain the same.  The map to the right is a map of the proposed MBTA Communities District created using Residensity. &nbsp;Residensity is an interactive mapping tool provided by the Massachusetts Housing Partnership&apos;s Center for Housing Data that explores and analyzes housing unit counts and density for any location in Massachusetts. &nbsp;This tool shows there are currently 1,980 housing units  that currently exist  within the proposed MBTA Communities District at a density of 16.3 housing units per acre. &nbsp;   The passage of the proposed MBTA Communities zoning would create the  capacity&nbsp; for 2,063 multifamily housing units at 21 units per acre. &nbsp;   The darker the shade of brown/red within the proposed MBTA Communities District, the more dense the parcel currently is. &nbsp;Parcels with no shading within the proposed MBTA Communities District do not have any housing units within them.  Other Zoning Changes     Another change to the text of the Zoning Ordinance has to do with standalone multifamily development in the CC/CC2 Zones. &nbsp;A multi-family development in these zones that does not meet certain criteria, such as providing a ground-floor commercial component, is only permitted by a Special Permit from the Planning Board. &nbsp;This only applies, however, in the  Core Pedestrian Area  (Cabot Street from Elliott Street to Highland Avenue, Rantoul Street from Bow Street to Edwards Street, and Railway Avenue and Broadway where they face Odell Park). &nbsp;Because the zoning for these types of multifamily developments are not by right, the properties within the Core Pedestrian Area cannot be included in the proposed MBTA Communities District. &nbsp;The properties in the Core Pedestrian Area along Cabot Street were purposefully excluded from the proposed MBTA Communities District because of this. &nbsp;The properties in the Core Pedestrian Area along Rantoul Street are to be included in the MMU, which allows for this type of development to offset the minimum multifamily housing unit capacity of the proposed MBTA Communities by 25%. &nbsp;The definition of Core Pedestrian Area needs to be revised to ensure that parcels that are in the MMU are within the Core Pedestrian Area and parcels that are in the proposed MBTA Communities District are outside of the Core Pedestrian Area. 
     

 
 
	   

	   

			    				
	 
	
	 	

 
    
	  	  
	   

	   
	 
	
	 	
 
	     


          Residensity mapping tool provided by the Massachusetts Housing Partnership shows that the Proposed MBTA Communiites District currently has 1,980 housing units at a density of 16.3 units per acre  
     

 
 
	   

	   

			      
		   				
	 
	
	 	
 
	     


         Summary 
     

 
 
	   

	   

			      
		   				
	 
	
	 	
 
	     


          Compliance with MBTA Communities Law is being sought by creating a Mandatory Mixed Use (MMU) District  The location of the MMU District has been approved by the Massachusetts Executive Office of Livable Communities and is located along Rantoul Street from Bow Street south to Edwards Street  The compliance model shows the proposed MBTA Communities Overlay District complies with the MBTA Communities Law using the 25% MMU offset and requires some Zoning revisions to the RHD and RMD lot size requirements and revisions to how lot sizes are calculated in the CC/CC2 zones.  Other changes to the Zoning Ordinance include:  Eliminating commercial parking requirements in the Depot Parking Overlay District  Redefining the Core Pedestrian Area
