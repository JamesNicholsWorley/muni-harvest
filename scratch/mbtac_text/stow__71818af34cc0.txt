Section 3A (“MBTA Communities”) FAQs 
The State’s MBTA Communities Law is a state mandate to adopt zoning, NOT to construct housing. 
Most Frequently Asked:  
1. What is included in the proposed bylaw to allow Stow to comply with the State law?  
• 
Density allowed through Zoning: The State requires up to15 units per acre (subject to well and 
septic requirements) 
• 
Location in Stow: East of Lower Village, along the north of Great Road between the Lower 
Village Cemetery and the Maynard town line.   
• 
Housing Types Allowed: Duplexes, triplexes, and townhomes with up to 5 units per structure. 
These housing types currently exist in Stow. The bylaw does not allow large apartment buildings. 
• 
Property Line Setbacks: The same as the underlying Residential District 
• 
Permitting Mechanisms Allowed: Includes a public hearing process through Site Plan Approval 
• 
Type of Zoning District: Overlay District (all existing uses, including single-family homes, are 
allowed to continue with no change needed or required) 
• 
Affordable Housing Allowances: 10% of housing may be deed-restricted for Affordable Housing.  
This is consistent with Stow’s existing Residential District. 
• 
Deadline to Adopt Compliant Zoning: December 31, 2025

includes support for infrastructure, public safety, open space, and recreation:

Over $500,000 for the Police Department 
•

5. Will the State or Town take land from property owners in the district to build multifamily 
housing?  
No, neither the State nor the Town will take land (known as eminent domain) from property owners. 
Property owners will continue to decide what happens to their land in the future, and whether or not it 
is developed. 
6. Isn’t this law intended to encourage transit-oriented design? Why isn’t the district near the South 
Acton train?  
Our district can be located anywhere in town. Stow does not have a commuter rail station and is 
considered an “adjacent small town” therefore the State decided to give adjacent small towns flexibility 
in locating the district anywhere in town, regardless of the distance to the train station. For those 
communities with a Commuter Rail located within their Town, the State is requiring housing to be 
located around the transit center. For towns like Stow, with no MBTA service, the State is encouraging 
additional housing to support business centers and village districts and to provide some walkability for 
those towns that do not have public transit. The State has reviewed Stow’s pre-adoption application and 
has confirmed the proposed location meets their requirements for compliance. 
 
Zoning for multifamily housing is only a piece of the continued effort for revitalizing Lower Village. 
Parcels near the South Acton train station came up in our public process, though we were unable to find 
enough qualifying land to create a district. More on the Board’s review of these locations can be found 
on page 14 of the Board’s review of alternative locations.  
 
7. Why not place the overlay district on commercial, business, or industrial land?  
Less than 5% of land in Stow remains available for commercial, business, or industrial purposes. 
Meaning, if we subtracted out land that is zoned for these purposes but is already used for housing 
developments, less than 5% of all land within Town is available for business. Using commercial, 
business, and industrial land for housing would conflict with the economic development goals of the 
Town, as indicated through past and recent planning processes. Having commercial, business, and 
industrial used land in a Town can have positive impacts on the local tax base, including less reliance 
on residential taxes and greater tax stability.  
 
8. How many new housing units will be built in Stow as a result of this new zoning? 
The legislation is a zoning exercise only; there is no mandate to build new housing. Property owners 
will decide if they want to develop their land or sell to a developer. Given that there are multiple 
property owners within the district, we anticipate any change will be gradual and at a smaller scale. 
Even the State acknowledges that for towns like Stow, any development is likely to be less than 15 
units/ acre due to well and septic requirements.  
 
Basics of SecƟon 3A 
1. What is an MBTA Community? 
An MBTA Community is one of 177 cities and towns in Massachusetts which either host an MBTA 
service or abut a city or town that hosts an MBTA service, therefore building off the principles of transit- 
oriented development. By State mandate, MBTA Communities must establish a zoning bylaw that 
allows multifamily housing at a density of 15 housing units per acre as of right.  
 
As of September 18, 2025, 141 cities and towns have passed zoning to comply with this legislation; 
80% of communities are in compliance, with additional communities holding Town Meetings this fall.

To create a zone that allows: 
• 
Minimum Density: The state requires up to 15 units per acre (can be an average across the district) 
• 
Minimum Unit Capacity: 139 housing units (5% of Stow’s housing units) 
•

•

to households making 80-100% of the Area Median Income. A greater percentage of deed-
restricted Affordable units or deeper affordability may be allowed only if supported by an 
economic feasibility analysis, paid for by the Town. 
• 
Other items of note: The zoning must not include age-restrictions nor limitations on the number 
of bedrooms, and must be suitable for families with children.

5. Is Section 3A an “Unfunded Mandate”?  
The State Auditor’s office argues that the MBTA Communities Act is an unfunded mandate. Opinions 
issued by the Division of Local Mandates are advisory in nature and have no legally binding effect. 
Governor Healey and AG Campbell have stated that the MBTA Communities Act is not an unfunded 
mandate and that the Auditor’s determination does not change the law’s enforceability.  
 
Every community (including Stow) that has requested funding for complying with the MBTA 
Communities Act to date has received it from the Executive Office of Housing and Livable 
Communities. The State has provided 157 out of 177 communities with more than $7.8 million in 
technical assistance grants to help officials draft new zoning districts, and further grant funding is still 
available from other entities.  
 
Updating zoning bylaws is a regular function of local government, so the MBTA Communities Act does 
not impose any additional direct costs on communities by requiring zoning changes.  
 
6. What is the State’s goal? What is the purpose of Section 3A? 
The State aims to address the housing shortage and grow a healthy and sustainable regional economy 
by requiring certain communities to offer zoning that allows a diversity of housing types. The State 
wishes to encourage walkable neighborhoods by having communities locate their multifamily housing 
zoning districts near businesses and services.  
 
The lack of zoning for multifamily housing is a barrier for developing new housing, particularly 
“missing middle” or “workforce” housing. Massachusetts has among the highest and fastest growing 
home prices and rents of any state in the nation. Rising costs have dramatically increased financial 
pressures on low and middle-income families, young families, single-income households, and our aging 
population, often forcing sacrifice of other priorities to pay for housing costs. 
 
By allowing multifamily housing in Stow, we can accomplish several goals laid out in the Master Plan, 
Housing Production Plan, Climate Action Plan, and Open Space and Recreation Plan: 
• 
Diversify housing options, including the provision of workforce housing, housing for empty-
nesters, adult children, and first-time homeowners; 
• 
Continue to emphasize small-scale solutions to affordable housing concerns; 
• 
Support community economic development by promoting infill housing in key economic centers; 
• 
Utilize creative zoning to locate new housing in suitable areas of Stow; 
• 
Encourage alternative modes of transportation to cut down local vehicle miles travelled 
 
7. What does “as of right” or “by-right” development mean? 
“As of right” or "by-right” means that Stow cannot require a Special Permit process to review proposed 
housing created through this zoning bylaw. A Special Permit is a discretionary permit considered 
through a public hearing process. Special Permits are typically required with housing developments. 
The Planning Board may still require a Site Plan Approval process, which includes a public hearing 
process. Construction would still require a building permit, any permitting through the Board of Health 
or Conservation Commission, submission of plans, and inspections. Individual property owners will 
have the option to decide if they want to redevelop their property under the new zoning or existing 
underlying zoning. No property owner is required to change their property or develop new housing.

8. What is multifamily housing?  
Defined by M.G.L. Ch.40A §1A, multifamily housing means a building with 3 or more residential 
dwelling units or 2 or more buildings on the same lot with more than 1 residential dwelling unit in each 
building. The Planning Board chose to limit this to duplexes, triplexes, and townhomes up to 5 units 
per structure (no large apartment complexes). 
 
9. Will this bylaw allow the construction of large apartment complexes? 
No, the Planning Board chose to limit the building types to duplexes, triplexes, and townhomes up to 5 
units per structure. 
 
10. Does this zoning mean that all parcels within the district will be constructed at a density of 15 
units per acre? 
No, just because the parcel is zoned to allow up to 15 housing units per acre, doesn’t mean that is what 
will be constructed- this is up to each individual owner. It’s also important to keep in mind the 
legislation is a zoning exercise only; there is no mandate to build new housing.  
 
A property owner will continue to have the rights they currently have, since the base zoning district 
will remain. Individual property owners would have the option to decide if they want to redevelop their 
property under the new zoning. No one is required to change their property or develop new housing. If 
a property owner chooses to develop their land, they may construct housing at a density up to 15 units 
per acre, meaning they could opt to construct at a lower density such as 3 units per acre or 8 units per 
acre, for example. Further, the zoning would open the door to allow conversion of a single-family home 
into a duplex or triplex.  
 
Other factors beyond zoning add further constraints to development, such as environmental regulations, 
public health regulations, real estate market demand, financing, and property owner preferences.  
 
11. Will this zoning allow the construction of high-rise buildings? 
The proposed bylaw includes a height restriction on multi-family units such that no building could 
exceed 35 feet in height (about 3 stories tall), which is currently an existing town-wide height 
restriction. The Planning Board chose to limit this to duplexes, triplexes, and townhomes up to 5 units 
per structure (no large apartment complexes). 
 
12. Is there a requirement to have some units set aside as deed-restricted Affordable Housing? 
The bylaw includes provisions for Affordable Housing consistent with any other residential 
development. The Board is looking into increasing this amount through a state-approved study. Any 
increase to Affordable Housing requirements will have to go back before Town Meeting for approval.  
 
13. Can we place age-restrictions on our MBTA Community district? 
No, Section 3A does not allow for multifamily housing to include a minimum age for any occupants. 
However, compliance with Section 3A is a zoning exercise only, so a developer could opt to place an 
age-restriction on housing within Stow’s district without impacting Stow’s 3A compliance. 
 
14. Can we limit the size or number of bedrooms in the housing units? 
No, Section 3A requires that multifamily housing is suitable for families, meaning that there cannot be 
age restrictions nor restrictions on the number of bedrooms, the size of bedrooms, and the number of 
occupants. However, multifamily housing will need to comply with Title 5 of the MA Environmental 
Code, which sets regulations on wells and septic systems that can limit the number of bedrooms 
developed.

23. What is an overlay district? 
An overlay zone or district is a zoning district that “lies” on top of existing zoning. It identifies special 
provisions in addition to those in the underlying zone.  The Planning Board is considering satisfying 
Stow’s Section 3A requirements via an overlay district, as opposed to altering underlying zoning.  Using 
this strategy, each property within the district would continue to have the existing base zoning in 
addition to the option of the 3A multifamily district zoning.

Infrastructure 
26. Will Stow need to install public utilities as a result of this zoning? What does this mean for water 
and sewer infrastructure? 
No, communities are not responsible for providing water, sewer, or other utilities to a proposed 
multifamily housing site. Housing developers would need to design projects that work within existing 
water and wastewater constraints. Title 5 would still apply to any future development, understanding 
that on-site septic is capped at 10,000 gallons per day (90 bedrooms). Otherwise, if the site has enough 
acreage, a wastewater treatment facility could be constructed by the developer, however this is very 
costly. While the zoning must allow for at least 15 units/acre, there may be limitations (such as a lack 
of infrastructure) that result in actual housing production at lower density than the zoning allows.  
 
27. Could a developer use utilities, like water and sewer, from a neighboring town?   
Other towns are not obligated to provide utilities to a project that is outside of their town, even if a 
property in Stow currently uses a neighboring town’s utilities. Maynard has indicated to staff that the 
town is not interested in supplying water to Stow as they are concerned with their own water capacity 
and quality.  
 
Local Impacts 
28. How will this impact open space or wetlands? 
One of the general principles of Section 3A is to “minimize impacts to sensitive land.” The legislation 
does not override state or local environmental regulations. New development that may occur within the 
multifamily district still needs to comply with all applicable state and local provisions. More 
specifically there is no public open space included in the proposed zoning changes. 
 
Section 3A is intended to reduce sprawl development and pollution by concentrating housing to 
minimize impacts to natural lands, farms, forests, and wetlands. 
 
According to MassWildlife’s Natural Heritage and Endangered Species program, there are no certified 
or potential vernal pools in the district. If there were vernal pools located in the district, they would be 
protected as they are across Stow.

31. Will this create a strain on our schools? 
Between the 2012-2013 school year and the 2022-2023 school year, the number of total students 
enrolled in the Nashoba Regional School District decreased by 10.2%, signaling a decline in the number 
of young children in Stow. Enrollment in the district peaked in the 2011-2012 school year with a total 
of 3,501 students, whereas the 2022-2023 school year had the lowest enrollment at 3,051 students. 
Socioeconomic projections indicate that this trend will continue in Stow in the future, with enrollment 
slowly declining in forecast years. Research shows multifamily housing increases school enrollment at 
a lower rate than single family homes. In the 2024-2025 school year, there were 21 Nashoba students 
at Pilot Grove (with 90 units).

allows Stow to benefit from multifamily housing, but at a pace and scale that is more aligned with Stow.

38. Will the new zoning lower home values? 
Many factors affect the value of residential property, and it is too early to determine if these 
requirements will actually create new housing, much less have a minor or significant influence on 
valuations. However, there is research showing that similar properties located in walkable 
neighborhoods or close to amenities, such as retail opportunities, have higher values than similar 
projects located at a distance.  
 
39. Can we control what multifamily housing looks like? 
Based on public outreach, Planning Board chose to limit this to duplexes, triplexes, and townhomes up 
to 5 units per structure (no large apartment complexes).We cannot control the specific design of any 
multifamily housing constructed under this bylaw. The zoning bylaw is able to call out limited specific 
components, such as building height, how far a new home is set back from the street, or the amount of 
parking required. For example, building height is limited to 35 feet, the same as the height restriction 
across town. 
 
40. I thought municipalities are able to change our own zoning! Why is the State stepping in? 
Massachusetts is a ‘home-rule’ state, meaning that towns may adopt, amend, or repeal local ordinances 
or bylaws, exercise any power or function which the general court has power to confer upon it “which 
is not inconsistent with the [state] constitution or laws enacted by the general court”. The Zoning Act 
already identifies many uses communities must allow including farms, churches, schools, childcare 
centers, and modifications to buildings to accommodate disability. Laws enacted by the general court 
can and do override local land use policies. Section 3A addresses the State’s housing shortage by 
increasing housing opportunities in our region.
