          Zoning for Multi-Family Housing - Requirements for MBTA Communities   Newbury&apos;s 2024 Annual Town Meeting was held on April 30, 2024. The Planning Board presented Article #14, proposing the MBTA Communities Multi-Family Zoning Overlay District. The Article was approved by Town Meeting voters, with 234 &quot;yes&quot; votes and 80 &quot;no&quot; votes.  Here is the  Final MBTA Communities Multi-Family Overlay District Bylaw (PDF)  as approved by Town Meeting.  Here is a  Map - MBTA Communities Multi-Family Districts (PDF)   The Massachusetts  Attorney General approved the bylaw and districts on August 19, 2024. (PDF)   The Merrimack Valley Planning Commission (MVPC) served as the Town&apos;s technical consultant in the development of the proposal. Ian Burns, MVPC&apos;s Community and Economic Development Program Manager,  presented these slides at Town Meeting (PDF) .  The Newbury Planning Board worked over the past year to gather public input and to prepare this Zoning Amendment for the Town to comply with the State&apos;s Section 3A/Multi-Family Zoning legislation. The proposal was discussed in public working meetings in October, November, December, and January. Public Hearings were held on February 21, February 28, and March 6, 2024. The amendment was revised and re-noticed for a Public Hearing held on March 20, 2024. Town Meeting approved the bylaw and districts as shown here:  Here is a  Fact sheet (PDF)  about MBTA zoning and the current Newbury proposal for compliance. Here are the  presentation (PDF)  slides that describe the Town&apos;s proposal. Here is the  video recording  from the final Public Information Session presentation by MVPC Planner Ian Burns, held at the Town Offices on Wednesday, April 24, 2024.  ALL of the public meetings, public hearings, and discussions can be viewed on the Archived  Planning Board meeting videos .  The zoning amendment was Article #14 on the Warrant for the 2024 Annual Town Meeting held on April 30, 2024, at 7 pm, at the Triton Regional High School.   &quot;MBTA Zoning&quot; or &quot;Section 3A&quot;: What is it?   In 2021, Massachusetts passed a new law, Section 3A of MGL c. 40A, requiring that an &quot;MBTA Community&quot; shall have at least one zoning district of reasonable size in which multi-family housing is permitted as of right and meets other criteria outlined in the statute. Multi-family housing is defined as 3 or more dwelling units in one structure. &quot;As of right&quot; or &quot;by right&quot; means that the use must be allowed without any special conditions or waivers. Other criteria for compliance include a minimum &quot;gross density&quot; of 15 units per acre as determined by the State&apos;s compliance model calculations.  It is important to note that this is a zoning mandate, NOT a housing production mandate. The legislation only requires that communities provide for multifamily housing zoning in their communities, not that they build it. Many other factors go into housing production, including roads, sewer, water supply, and infrastructure. Any housing production using the Multi-Family Overlay District will have to comply with all state and local infrastructure requirements, including wetlands protection and Title V septic requirements, as well as ensuring the provision of sufficient water supply for the residences.   What is an &quot;MBTA Community&quot;?  Newbury is an &quot;Adjacent small-town community&quot; located near the Newburyport MBTA train station. Read more about how the regulations  apply to Newbury .   What will happen if Newbury does NOT comply?  If Newbury does not comply with the 3A legislation, the Town risks several penalties as outlined in the statute and guidelines written by the Executive Office of Housing and Livable Communities (EOHLC). Section 3A states that non-compliant communities will be ineligible for certain grant programs: MassWorks Infrastructure Grants, Housing Choice Community Grants, and the Local Capital Projects Funds. In addition, non-compliant communities will be less competitive when applying for 12 other categories of state grant programs. In recent years, Newbury has received more than $1.7 million in State grant funding. For further explanation, read the memo from  MVPC consultants (PDF) .   How is Newbury planning to comply?  An amendment to the Town&apos;s Zoning Bylaw will be considered by the voters on the  Warrant for the 2024 Annual Town Meeting on April 30, 2024 (PDF) . The Massachusetts Executive Office of Housing and Livable Communities (EOHLC, formerly DHCD) has issued guidelines for compliance with Section 3A. Read &nbsp;the letter (PDF)  sent to each MBTA Community from Secretary Mike Kennealy and Undersecretary Jennifer Maddox. These are  the guidelines  that towns, including Newbury, must follow to comply.  Newbury applied for and received a grant from Mass Housing Partnership to hire consultants for technical assistance. Staff from the Merrimack Valley Planning Commission (MVPC) have been providing this technical support since the Summer of 2023 through the Spring of 2024. Initial ideas around districts and parcels to include in Newbury&apos;s multi-family zoning overlay district were presented to the community during the October 18, 2023, Planning Board meeting.  View the slides (PDF)  from this October 2023 presentation by staff from the Merrimack Valley Planning Commission.  Additional meetings have included two Planning Board in-person workshops with technical support from MVPC staff on November 13, 2023 and January 3, 2024; a joint Planning Board and Select Board meeting on December 6, 2023, where an  updated presentation (PDF)  was given by MVPC; and a Public Forum at the Newbury Library on December 11, 2023, which reviewed these  12/11/23 slides (PDF) . Planning Board meetings in January and February continued the discussions, and multiple Public Hearings were held in February and March as described above. To review the discussions held at any of these meetings, please visit the Town&apos;s  recorded meeting videos . Written minutes are also posted on the Town website as they are approved.   Why do we need more multi-family housing? Why is building this housing near transit important?    Massachusetts is in a housing crisis, with some of the highest and fastest-growing home prices and rents of any state in the nation.  Rising costs have dramatically increased financial pressures on low- and middle-income families, forcing them to sacrifice other priorities to pay housing costs. High housing costs are a primary driver of homelessness.  These high costs are a disadvantage as we compete economically against peer states. The risk of future job growth moving outside Massachusetts is rising due to the high cost of living.  The lack of zoning for multi-family housing is a barrier to new housing development in Massachusetts.  By allowing multifamily housing near transit, we can create new housing in walkable neighborhoods closer to transit. This is not just good housing policy, it is good climate and transportation policy, too.    What happens when zoning reform allows for more housing to be built?  Housing becomes more affordable. In local communities across Massachusetts, as MBTA/3A Zoning is being discussed, we should keep this in mind. One outcome of approving the zoning changes will likely be more affordable rents for everyone!  View the research from the Pew Charitable Trusts .   How does the production of multi-family housing affect school enrollment?  The concern that constructing new housing will drive up school enrollment is long-standing and widespread in local debates about new residential development and zoning reform in Massachusetts, especially in suburban communities. Many local officials and residents assume that new housing, and especially new multifamily housing, will attract families with children who will inevitably increase enrollment in the local public schools, thus burdening school budgets and municipal finances. A recent report by MAPC (February 2024) helps answer these questions:  An update on housing production&apos;s effect on public school enrollment.
