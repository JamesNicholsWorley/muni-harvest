B. By adding new Section 300-4.7 to read as follows: 
 
§ 300-4.7 
Additional Use Regulations for the 3A-MFO District    
 
A.   
The purpose of the 3A-MFO Overlay District is to allow multifamily housing development in 
accordance with G.L. c. 40A, § 3A and the Section 3A Compliance Guidelines of the Executive 
Office of Housing and Livable Communities (HLC), as may be amended from time to time. In 
addition, Section 300-4.7 is intended to encourage:  
 
1. 
New or redeveloped housing in close proximity to the Cohasset commuter rail station; 
 

[PAGE 2]
Z3: 3A Multi-Family Overlay District 
 
03/19/2024 
2. A mix of housing sizes and types in walkable areas to promote public health, reduce 
greenhouse gases, improve air quality, and concentrate development where adequate facilities 
and services exist to support it;  
 
3. New investment in housing and mixed-use developments in the Overlay District 
 
B.   Unless otherwise regulated within this Section 300-4.7, all use, dimension, and all other 
provisions of the Zoning Bylaw shall remain in full force. Where the 3A-MFO authorizes uses 
not otherwise allowed in the underlying district, the provisions of the 3A-MFO shall control. 
 
C. All projects proposed within the 3A-MFO District shall be subject to Site Plan Review before 
the Planning Board pursuant to Section 12.7 herein, however, such Section 12.7 to be applied 
and construed in a manner consistent with Section 4A of the Compliance Guidelines of the 
Executive Office of Housing and Livable Communities (HLC), as may be amended from time to 
time. 
 
D. All projects proposed within the 3A-MFO District shall be subject to all other applicable 
General and Zoning Bylaws of the Town, including, but not limited to, Chapter 223 of the 
General Bylaws (Stormwater Management). 
 
E.  Uses. 
 
(1) Multi-Family Use within the 3A-MFO District is allowed as of right.  
(2) Mixed Use is encouraged to be developed as part of a project within the 3A-MFO District 
especially if a project proposed replaces a pre-existing commercial use.  
(a) Commercial uses, if constructed, shall be constructed on the first floor of Buildings. 
(b) The allowed Commercial uses in a Mixed-Use project in the 3A-MFO District would be the 
same Retail, Office and Service uses allowed in the underlying 3A-C District, and additionally 
Home Occupation, and Government Service Provider.

         As of 05/07/2025 Cohasset has received District Compliance&nbsp;  Project Background   This new law requires that an MBTA community shall have at least one zoning district of reasonable size in which multi-family housing is permitted as of right and meets other criteria set forth in the statute:     Minimum gross density of 15 units per acre    Located not more than 0.5 miles from a commuter rail station, subway station, ferry terminal or bus station, if applicable    No age restrictions and suitable for families with children     In 2020, Governor Baker signed into law a requirement that Cohasset, along with 174 other communities in eastern Massachusetts with access to MBTA service, create a district or districts were multifamily housing is allowed by right. Updated guidelines from the Executive Office of Housing and Livable Communities (Formerly DHCD, now EOHLC) indicate that Cohasset is an &quot;Commuter Rail&quot; community, and 50% of the new zoning district(s) need to be within proximity of the Commuter Rail Station.  More information from the state regarding this law can be found here .    On March 15, 2023, the   Attorney General issued an advisory concerning enforcement of the law  . By complying with this requirement, the Town will:     remain eligible for important state grants for infrastructure projects;    take part in a regional response to the region&apos;s housing crisis by encouraging development of smaller multifamily housing          Throughout 2023, the Town, supported by resident volunteers in the Zoning Bylaw Committee, has been leading a planning process with to determine the location and details of Cohasset&apos;s district(s).  &nbsp;    The deadline for Cohasset to comply with this law is December 31st, 2024. Our hope is to have the new overlay district accepted at Annual Town Meeting June 3rd, 2024.

Article 2: Opioid Settlement Fund.  To see what sum Town Meeting will vote to transfer from Free Cash 
for programs and projects set out in the Massachusetts State-Subdivision Agreement for Statewide Opioid 
Settlements and the National Opioid Settlement Agreement to combat the opioid epidemic, or take any 
other action thereon. 
 
This article seeks to appropriate funds the Town has received through the Massachusetts State-Subdivision 
Agreement for Statewide Opioid Settlements and the National Opioid Settlement Agreement.  Although 
these funds must be used for purposes outlined in the agreements, such as substance use disorder pre-
vention, harm reduction, treatment, and recovery, Massachusetts finance law in Fiscal Year 2023 treated 
the settlement payouts as general fund revenue subject to appropriation.  Through the end of Fiscal Year 
2023, the Town had received $178,522.45.  We recommend appropriating $178,522.45 from available Free 
Cash to begin this work. In Fiscal Year 2024 and beyond, the State Legislature and Department of Revenue 
have amended the process so that these funds can be spent as they are received.

Article 3: School Transportation Vehicles.  To see if Town Meeting will vote to amend the May 2023 Annual 
Town Meeting Article 12 to allow funding to be used for the purchase of school vehicles and related equip-
ment, or take any other action thereon.   
 
Based on current supply and district need, the School Department believes it prudent to purchase a school 
bus (rather than two passenger vans) using the $150,000 appropriated in May 2023. Due to the specificity 
of the original article, an amendment to authorize this purchase is required. Broadening the language al-
lows greater flexibility to Public Works and the School Department in maintaining the appropriate fleet. 
 
Article 4: Building & Electrical Permit Fees.  To see if Town Meeting will vote to amend Chapters V and 
IX.A of the General By-law, or take any other action  thereon. 
 
Every three to five years, the Inspectional Services office reviews its fees to ensure their constistency with 
(a) the amount of staff time it takes to administer such permits and (b) similar fees across the region.  
Chapter V (Inspector of Wires) was last updated in 2013, while Chapter IX.A (Building Permits) was last 
updated in 2019.   
Based on staff’s most recent review of building and electrical permit fees, the following changes are rec-
ommended: 
- 
Adjust the Town minimum fees from $25 to $50 in both chapters. 
- 
Make residential permit fees consistent throughout the bylaw at $10 per thousand (currently the 
fees range from $8 per thousand to $12 per thousand depending on the type of work being done) 
- 
Adjust by $0.50 per thousand the basic electrical permit fees for single- and two-family homes 
- 
Adjust by $1 per thousand the electric permit fee for multi-family and non-residential 
- 
Adjust other fees associated with larger projects requiring higher amperage or temporary services   
- 
Delete reference to the non-residential fee structure adopted as part of the 2012 Essex North 
Shore Agricultural and Technical High School building project, the fees for which were discounted 
as part of the $140 million project 
Secondarily, this review identified several desirable minor changes pertaining to formatting and grammar 
that would improve the clarity of these Chapters and are included in the proposed article.  To improve the 
readability of the documents, the housekeeping changes include adding section titles, removing a duplica-
tive paragraph, correcting departmental and position titles, and addressing grammatical issues. 
A full copy of the proposal, including a redlined version of the proposed amendment, is available on the 
Town Clerk’s page of the website: https://www.danversma.gov/799/Town-Meeting-Warrants.

Article 5: Pool Fencing.  To see if Town Meeting will vote to amend Chapter XX of the General By-Laws, 
as follows, or take any other action thereon. 
 
This Chapter, first adopted in 1975, when State law was silent on pool safety requirements, has never been 
amended.  Since that time, the Commonwealth’s Building Code introduced its own safety standards, which 
created a conflict between the two laws.  This has led to difficulty for residents who can incur unexpected 
expenses satisfying both requirements.  This amendment addresses this by striking the inconsistent lan-
guage to mirror the State’s, as shown below.  Deletions are struck through, and insertions are underlined. 
All permanently installed private swimming pools and all other pools capable of retaining 
over two-and-one-half feet of water shall be fully enclosed by a suitable fence or an equiv-
alent enclosure, or means of protection from access to the pool as defined in the Massa-
chusetts State Building Code, not less than fourfive feet in height and containing a self-
latching gate to ensure that small children will be barred from the swimming pool area.  
A full copy of the proposal, including a redlined version of the proposed amendment, is available on the 
Town Clerk’s page of the website: https://www.danversma.gov/799/Town-Meeting-Warrants.  
 
Article 6: Easement at 42 Summer Street.  To see if Town Meeting will vote (1)  to authorize the Select 
Board to grant to C.P. Berry Residences, LLC, on such terms as the Select Board deems appropriate, an 
easement within an area containing three hundred twenty nine (329) square feet, more or less, shown as 
“Easement Area=329+/- S.F.” on a plan entitled “Exhibit Easement Plan of Land in Danvers, MA, Prepared 
for C.P. Berry Residences, LLC”; prepared by Hancock Survey Associates, Inc.; 185 Centre Street, Danvers, 
MA 01923, Scale: 1”=20’; Date 7/19/23; and to authorize the Select Board to take all action necessary to 
accomplish the purposes of this article; or take any other action thereon. 
 
The easement area in question is located on Town of Danvers property, which abuts the property at 42 
Summer Street.  The structure on said property is known as the James Putnam, Jr. House, which is listed 
on the National Register of Historic Places.  The purpose of this grant of easement is to allow said James 
Putnam, Jr. House to continue to encroach, in part, within the layout of Summer Street, a public way, but 
not on the travelled way of Summer Street, and not on the sidewalk within the layout of Summer Street.  
As the structure in question predated both moden zoning and the layout of Summer Street by at least a 
century, staff strongly supports this easement request.  Please refer to Exhibit A in your warrant booklets 
for a map depicting the impacted area. 
 
Article 7: Adaptive Reuse.  To see if Town Meeting will vote to amend Section 30 of the Zoning By-Law, 
or take any other action thereon. 
 
At the 2023 Annual Town Meeting, a study committee (with representatives from the Finance Committee, 
Planning Board, Zoning Board, Preservation Commission, and Historic District Commission) was created to 
review this and other related preservation concepts.   
 

[PAGE 4]

“Adaptive reuse” refers to re-using an existing building for a purpose other than for what it was originally 
built or designed.  This can be a viable and attractive alternative to demolition or new construction, while 
retaining historic features of the original building.  In 2022, the Planning office forwarded a similar proposal 
as an option for the building at 42 Summer Street, which was slated for demolition as part of a larger 
project.  Ultimately, demolition of that building was avoided when a buyer emerged with plans to restore 
the structure. 
The formation of the Study Committee in 2023 presented an opportunity to revisit the concept, as there 
are dozens of other pre-1890 structures in Danvers that may be suitable candidates for Adaptive Reuse.  
This amendment would allow property owners of such structures to request dimensional relief (e.g., set-
backs or uses, through a special permit before the Zoning Board).  The Study Committee recommends the 
approval of this version of the proposed by-law. 
A full copy of the proposal, including a redlined version of the proposed amendment, is available on the 
Town Clerk’s page of the website: https://www.danversma.gov/799/Town-Meeting-Warrants.  
 
Article 8: MBTA Communities Act Compliance.  To see if Town Meeting will vote to amend the Zoning 
Bylaw to adjust the minimum densities, parking ratios, and related provisions in the Character Based Zon-
ing Districts to comply with the MBTA Communities Act and its implementing regulations, or take any 
other action thereon. 
 
The MBTA Communities Law was passed in January 2021.  The law intends to address Massachusetts’ hous-
ing shortage by requiring all MBTA Communities, of which Danvers is one of 177, to allow multifamily hous-
ing by right.  Fortunately, the downtown zoning adopted by Town Meeting in February 2020 was informed 
by a draft version of the law, the article passed at Town Meeting, and remains compliant with the Act.  
What needs to be addressed now stems from details included in the implementing regulations, which were 
not finalized until August 2023. 
The proposal under this article amends the downtown zoning districts to satisfy the Commonwealth’s reg-
ulations regarding the minimum densities for multi-family dwellings and the parking rules.  While these 
changes are significant, their impact is not projected to dramatically impact the overall build-out of the 
corridor.  This is partly because the state’s regulations affect the minimum number of units allowed in the 
district rather than the maximum.   
This proposal was workshopped extensively by the Planning Board throughout the fall, with Town Meeting 
members invited to participate on all occasions.  The feedback was instructive, and the proposal has been 
adjusted based on public feedback. 
Failure to fully comply with the MBTA Communities Act would disqualify the Town from participating in 
certain grant programs. e.g. MassWorks, which several years ago provided the Town with $1 million grant 
used to extend the Danvers Rail Trail toward Middleton. 
A full copy of the proposal, including a redlined version of the proposed amendment, is available on the 
Town Clerk’s page of the website: https://www.danversma.gov/799/Town-Meeting-Warrants.

Article 9: Housekeeping.  To see if Town Meeting will vote to amend the Zoning Bylaw to to improve 
internal consistency in language, provide clarity where vague, conform to state or case law, and move all 
signage standards to one place, or take any other action thereon. 
 
As the warrant language suggests, staff has noted several instances where the bylaw's language could be 
amended to improve the readability and clarity of the By-Law.  These changes are meant to minimize con-
fusion and/or enforcement actions in the daily administration of the zoning rules.  Changes include: 
• 
Reorganize Section 3, General Provisions, to improve the language around non-conformities.  There 
is no change in policy here. 
• 
Section 4, Site Plan Review.  This section is amended to parallel many elements of special permits, 
such as abutter notification and action deadlines. 
• 
Clarify language in Section 9 (Accessory Uses) and Section 10 (Off Street Parking Standards). 
• 
Merge language regarding the affordable housing provisions into one section while addressing an er-
ror in the formula. 
• 
Clarify the language of Section 30, Special Permits. 
• 
Move all signage requirements into Section 37, Signage.  Based on business feedback, this also in-
cludes allowing temporary signs, typically grand opening or special promotion signage, to remain in 
place for forty-five (45) days rather than ten (10) days. 
• 
Correct any grammatical errors or terminology changes throughout the By-Law to ensure consistency. 
A full copy of the proposal, including a redlined version of the proposed amendment, is available on the 
Town Clerk’s page of the website: https://www.danversma.gov/799/Town-Meeting-Warrants.

OVERVIEW
What is the MBTA Communities Act?
Massachusetts created a requirement that each of the 177 designated MBTA Communities in the
Commonwealth must develop a zoned district that allows multi-family housing “as of right.” 
(References: MGL c.40A Section 3A; MGL c.161A Section 1)
What does “as of right” mean?
The term “as of right” in zoning means that a proposed development project may proceed 
according to a zoning bylaw without the need for a special permit, variance, zoning amendment, or
other discretionary zoning approval. Notably “as of right” does allow for a site plan review process 
as well as reviews by all relevant boards, commissions and departments to ensure that the 
development adheres to Dover’s existing bylaws and regulations.
What is the definition of “multi-family” under the law?
“Multi-family” refers to a building with three or more residential units, or two or more buildings on 
the same lot with more than one residential unit in each building. The definition encompasses a 
range of housing types, including townhomes, triple-deckers, and single-family cluster-style 
developments.
Why was Dover designated as an MBTA Community?
Even though Dover does not have MBTA services within its borders, because it abuts a town that 
does provide services, it has been deemed to be a “Small Adjacent Community” and so must 
comply with the law.

The key differences between Chapter 40B and the MBTA Communities zoning requirement in

Chapter 40B
Allows developers to override local zoning bylaws and regulations if a town has less than 
10% affordable housing stock, in order to build affordable housing developments.
At least 20-25% of units in the development must be
income-restricted as affordable.

locally in

Applies statewide to all cities and towns that don't meet the 10% affordable housing

MBTA Communities Zoning (Section 3A)
Requires MBTA communities to have at least one zoning district allowing multi-family 
housing as-of-right near transit stations.
Does not mandate a specific percentage of affordable units, but the housing must be suitable
for families.
Applies only to the 177 cities and towns served by the MBTA transit system.
Non-compliant communities become ineligible for certain state housing, infrastructure and 
transportation funding programs.
Aims to promote transit-oriented housing development near public transit to increase housing
supply.
In summary, 40B is a state override to require affordable housing production, while the 
MBTA zoning law requires zoning for multi-family housing near transit in communities served
by the MBTA, regardless of their existing affordable housing stock levels

What is zoning?

Will a Zoning Overlay District be used, and if so, what does that mean?
A Zoning Overlay gives property developers the choice to follow either the Overlay District rules 
or the original Underlying Zoning rules. For instance, an MBTA Overlay District would introduce 
new development guidelines that developers can choose to follow. An Overlay District is a type of
land use zoning district that "lies" on top of the existing zoning and potentially covers many 
underlying districts or portions thereof. The use of Overlay Districts provides communities with an
opportunity to meet the unique needs of geographic areas that may either require higher  levels of
protection or that may not be reaching their fullest development potential. Overlay Districts 
provide one of the most flexible means to alter zoning as they can impose either greater 
restrictions or more flexible provisions depending on the goals for the district. Examples of typical 
overlays that are more restrictive include those for sensitive resources such as flood plains or 
aquifer protection districts. Examples of typical overlays that are more flexible include those for 
village centers, or other areas designated for higher levels of density and economic development.
An example of an existing overlay district in Dover is the Large-Scale Solar District which is 
located on Powisset Street by the Town Transfer Station

Here   is a list of actions take prior to 2025 to maintain interim compliance with the MBTA Communities Act.  How is the MBTA Communities Act different from Chapter 40B?  The key differences between Chapter 40B and the MBTA Communities zoning requirement in Massachusetts are:  MBTA Communities Act (Section 3A): emphasis is on multi-family housing    Requires MBTA communities to have at least one zoning district allowing multi-family housing as-of-right.    Does not mandate the inclusion of affordable units, but the housing must be suitable for families.    Applies only to the 177 cities and towns served by the MBTA transit system.    Non-compliant communities become ineligible for certain state housing, infrastructure and transportation funding programs.    Aims to promote transit-oriented housing development near public transit to increase housing supply.    Allows Dover to control where and what, from a design perspective, gets developed.    Chapter 40B: emphasis is on affordable housing    Allows developers to override local zoning bylaws and regulations if a town has less than 10% affordable housing stock, in order to build affordable housing developments. Once approved by the State, Dover cannot reject a development except through legal action.    At least 20-25% of units in the affordable housing development must be income-restricted as affordable.    Developers can appeal to the state Housing Appeals Committee if their project is denied locally in non-compliant towns.    Applies statewide to all cities and towns that don&apos;t meet the 10% affordable housing threshold. Dover does not currently meet this threshold.    For more information on Chapter 40B in Dover, please visit our 40B information page   here   .

Dracut MBTA Communities Survey
Section 3A of Massachusetts General Laws Chapter 40A requires that Dracut and 176 other Massachusetts 
communities provide at least one zoning district of reasonable size in which multi family housing appropriate for 
families is permitted "as-of-right." Dracut must adopt this zoning no later than December 31, 2024.
The Zoning Bylaw Review Committee, working with the Planning Board and Board of Selectmen, is working to create a 
compliant district for Town Meeting consideration in Fall of 2024.
This survey is intended for Dracut residents to provide initial feedback to the Zoning Bylaw Review Committee. We 
appreciate your time to answer the following questions and provide input on this process. This survey will be open 
until March 6, 2024, after which time the Zoning Bylaw Review Committee will host subsequent virtual and in-person 
meetings to discuss the results and compliance for the Town of Dracut.
For more information on this law, the process, and to attend the virtual community meeting on February 29, please 
visit www.dracutma.gov/MBTA-Zoning
If you would like to receive email updates on the Town of Dracut MBTA Compliance process please sign up using the

The Three Options
The Zoning Bylaw Review Committee has proposed three options for an MBTA Communities 
Overlay District. We are calling these options "Collinsville and Tennis Plaza", "Broadway/Loon Hill 
and Tennis Plaza", and "Broadway/Loon Hill and Navy Yard".
Collinsville and Tennis Plaza
This area includes the Collinsville Mill and 
residential parcels around Tennis Plaza. This option 
would allow additional residential development in 
Collinsville Mill and longer-term division or 
redevelopment of nearby properties into smaller 
multifamily buildings. New homes that may be 
eventually developed would be within walking 
distance to Dracut High School and the businesses 
in and near the mill.

[PAGE 4]
Broadway/Loon Hill and Tennis Plaza
This area does not include Collinsville Mill, and instead 
pairs the parcels around the Tennis Plaza with parcels near 
the Broadway and Loon Hill Road intersection. This option 
would allow larger developments set back from the road in 
the Broadway/Loon Hill area, possibly mixed with 
commercial areas. New homes that may be eventually 
developed would be within walking distance to Veterans 
Park, Dracut High School, and many businesses.
Broadway/Loon Hill and Navy Yard
Instead of the Tennis Plaza area, this area pairs 
Broadway/Loon Hill with the Navy Yard Mill and 
surrounding neighborhood. This option would allow 
additional residential development in the Navy Yard Mill 
and longer-term division or redevelopment of nearby 
properties into smaller multifamily buildings. New homes 
that may be eventually developed would be within walking 
distance to the businesses in the Navy Yard neighborhood 
along with the businesses near Veterans Park.
