9.10 MBTA Communities Multi-family Overlay District 
 
9.10.1 Intent and Purpose: 
The purpose of the MBTA Communities Multi-family Overlay District (MCMOD) is to allow multi-family 
housing as of right in accordance with Section 3A of the Zoning Act (Massachusetts General Laws Chapter 
40A). This zoning allows for as of right multi-family housing to encourage: 
1. The production of a variety of housing types that will provide equal access to new housing for people 
with a variety of needs and income levels. 
2. The development of housing that is supportive of public transit, pedestrian, and bicycle travel.  
 
9.10.2 Establishment: 
The MCMOD is an overlay district that is superimposed over the underlying zoning district, consisting of 
Parcels 63, 64, and 64.1 on Assessor’s Map 15 as of January 1, 2025, and is shown on the Town’s official 
Zoning Map.  
 
9.10.3 Definitions: 
 
AFFORDABLE RESIDENTIAL DWELLING UNIT. A housing unit that is subject to a use restriction 
recorded in its chain of title limiting the sale price or rent or limiting occupancy to an individual or 
household of a specified income, or both. 
AFFORDABLE HOUSING. Housing that contains Affordable Residential Dwelling Units. 
APPLICANT. A person, business, or organization that applies for a Building Permit, Site Plan Review, 
Special Permit, Variance, Waiver or other zoning approval.  
AS OF RIGHT. Development that may proceed under the Zoning in place at time of application without 
the need for a special permit, variance, zoning amendment, waiver, or other discretionary zoning 
approval.  
BUILDING COVERAGE. The maximum area of the lot that can be attributed to the footprint of the 
buildings (principal and accessory) on that lot. Building Coverage does not include surface parking.

EOHLC. The Massachusetts Executive Office of Housing and Community Development, or any

MBTA. Massachusetts Bay Transportation Authority. 
 
MULTI-FAMILY HOUSING. A building with three or more residential dwelling units or two or more 
buildings on the same lot with more than one residential dwelling unit in each building.  
 
MULTI-FAMILY ZONING DISTRICT. A zoning district, either a base district or an overlay district, in 
which multi-family housing is allowed as of right.  
 
PARKING, SURFACE. One or more parking spaces without a built structure above the space.  
 
SECTION 3A. Section 3A of M.G.L. Chapter 40A, the MA Zoning Act. 
 
DWELLING, TWO FAMILY.  A detached residential building on a lot designed, intended, and used 
exclusively as the residence of two families. A two-family dwelling shall not include a detached single-
family dwelling with an accessory dwelling unit. 
 
9.10.4 Applicability of MCMOD: 
An Applicant may develop multi-family housing located within a MCMOD in accordance with the 
provisions of this Section 9.10.  
 
9.10.4.1  Underlying Zoning.  
The MCMOD is an overlay district superimposed on underlying zoning districts. The regulations for 
use, dimension, and all other provisions of the Zoning By-law governing the respective underlying 
zoning district(s) shall remain in full force, except for uses allowed as of right or by special permit in 
the MCMOD. Uses that are not identified in Section 9.10 are governed by the requirements of the 
underlying zoning district(s).

District (MCMOD). The Planning Board shall act as the administering authority for any Site Plan Review 
procedure associated with this bylaw. Where standards or other requirements listed as part of this overlay 
district may conflict with those in the underlying district, the MCMOD overlay standards and provisions 
shall apply.

To date, the community has continued to study the current housing market
and demands in Ashland and the MetroWest region while planning for the
future. Ashland with the help of the Metropolitan Area Planning Council in
2021 completed an update to the community's Housing Production Plan in
a two-step housing project. First, the HPP was completed and accepted by
the Commonwealth, however, the second step proposing the adoption of
an inclusionary zoning bylaw is a work in process. Ashland's housing goals
are to foster the continued growth of all housing types ranging from
single-family residential to affordable senior housing.
Ashland's continued work studying housing needs and setting future goals
has included MBTA (3A) zoning. Focusing on MBTA Zoning, Ashland has
done a self-assessment of the current zoning surrounding the Ashland
commuter rail station. There are five zoning districts and seven sub-districts
within a half mile of the Ashland Commuter Rail Station. These districts
include Residential A (RA), Residential B (RB), Industrial (IND), Ashland
Downtown Districts sub-districts A and C (ADD-A and ADD-C), and all
sub-districts in the Rail Transit District (RTD-A through RTD-E). Of these
10 districts and sub-districts, only ADD-A substantially meets the accepted
guidelines. Other zoning districts in the .5-mile radius could meet the MBTA
guidelines with small amendments, while the remaining districts would
need substantial reworking to meet the requirements.
Furthermore, Ashland is at the beginning stages of updating the town's
master plan. This past fall the community funded a new master planning
process last completed in 2003. This cumbersome planning effort has
started internally and the community plans to publish an RFP in the spring
of 2023. The community-wide master planning process will incorporate
MBTA zoning shaping the area around the Ashland commuter rail station
for the decades to come.

MBTA Communities - Frequently Asked Questions 
 
1. What is the MBTA Communities Act? 
• 
The MBTA Communities Act was passed in January 2021 and requires communities that 
host an MBTA station, host a T stop, or are “adjacent communities”, to zone for 
additional multi-family housing by right based on the community’s Housing Stock.  
 
2. What towns and cities do this law apply to?  
•

3. What are the basic Multi-Family Zoning Requirements for MBTA Communities for Ashland? 
• 
Housing Units:  As a Commuter Rail Community, Ashland has to zone for a number of 
dwelling units equal to 15% of its existing housing units, or 1,124 units.  This does not 
mean that the units need to be built. 
• 
Land Area:  The MBTA zoned land area has to be a minimum of 50 acres. 
• 
Density:  Zoned areas must have a minimum density of 15 units per acre.  
 
4. Are there any maximum density requirements? 
• 
There is no required maximum density, but the State’s zoning model takes building 
height, setbacks and parking into consideration.  The site must be able to actually 
accommodate the proposed density. 
 
5. If we adopt the recommended configuration in Ashland, what areas will be impacted? 
• 
The proposed bylaw has 5 very specific sub-districts where MBTA multi-family housing 
could be located.  The MBTA zoning would only pertain to these specific areas, which 
total approximately 56 acres of Ashland’s 7,936 land acres. 
 
6. What’s the difference between the 40B law and MBTA Communities requirements? 
• 
Chapter 40B was enacted in 1969 to help address the shortage of affordable housing.  It 
allows developers that pursue the 40B process to bypass local zoning requirements in 
exchange for providing 25% of the total units at affordable prices.  Chapter 40B projects 
are allowed when a community does not have at least 10% of its total housing units set 
aside as affordable.   
• 
The MBTA Communities Act was signed into law in 2021 and focuses on zoning, not 
individual development projects.  MBTA Communities requires communities with or 
adjacent to commuter rail stations or T stops to zone for multi-family housing near 
MBTA stations.  See the attached chart for additional information.

7. Can MBTA Zoning include land that is already built on? 
• 
Yes, zoning only considers the land beneath a site, not what is currently built (or not 
built) on it.  MBTA zoning can include empty parcels or ones that already have buildings 
on them.  
 
8. What happens if Ashland does not adopt MBTA Communities requirements? 
• 
If Ashland does not comply with the guidelines of MBTA Communities, Ashland would 
be ineligible for certain state housing, infrastructure, and transportation funding 
programs. Non-compliant communities could also be liable for litigation. There is 
currently litigation between the Commonwealth and the Town of Milton, which has 
opted not to comply.  A precedent decision will likely be issued by the Massachusetts 
Supreme Judicial Court (SJC) in the upcoming weeks.

320-8.7 MBTA Multifamily Overlay District 
A. Purposes. The purposes of the MBTA Multifamily Overlay District (MOD) are to:  
1. Provide for multifamily housing development or mixed-use development in accordance 
with G.L. c. 40A, § 3A, and the Compliance Guidelines for Multi-family Zoning Districts 
Under Section 3A of the Zoning Act (the Guidelines) issued by the Executive Office of 
Housing and Livable Communities (EOHLC);  
2.   Encourage the production of a variety of housing sizes and types to provide access to new housing 
for people with a variety of needs and income levels; and 
3.   Increase the tax base through private investment in new housing development. 
B. Establishment of District 
The MOD is shown on the map entitled, MBTA Multifamily Overlay District, and dated [date], on file 
with the Town Clerk.  
 
C. Applicability.  
Development of land within the MOD may be undertaken subject to the requirements of this Section 8.7 
or by complying with all applicable requirements of the underlying district. Developments proceeding 
under this Section 8.7 shall be governed solely by the provisions herein and the standards or procedures of 
the underlying districts shall not apply. Where the MOD provides for uses and dimensional controls not 
otherwise allowed in the underlying district and/or applicable overlay districts, the provisions of the MOD 
shall control. Uses and dimensional controls of the MOD are not subject to any special permit 
requirements of the underlying district and/or applicable overlay districts.  
 
The MOD contains the following sub-districts, all of which are shown on the Multifamily Overlay 
District map: 
 
Subdistrict 1/West Ayer Village FBC 
Subdistrict 2/Downtown/Park Street FBC 
 
D. Use Regulations 
The following uses shall be permitted in the MOD, subject to MOD Development Plan Review under 
Section 8.7(I).  Section 3.5, Site Plan Review, shall not apply to developments under this Section 8.7. 
 
1. Multifamily housing. For purposes of this Section 8.7, multifamily housing includes a building with 
three or more residential dwelling units containing housing suitable for families as defined in the 
EOHLC Guidelines or two or more buildings on the same lot with more than one residential dwelling 
unit in each building. 
2. Accessory uses 
a) Community building

Bedford Housing Partnership Urge Support for Proposed Bylaw to     
Comply with State MBTA Communities Act   
The Bedford Housing Partnership (Housing Partnership | Bedford, MA (bedfordma.gov), at its meeting in 
January 16, 2024,  urged support for the proposed bylaw that will be presented to voters at  the 
Bedford Town Meeting on March 25th.  Passage of the bylaw is needed to comply with the new 
Massachusetts MBTA Communities Act known generally as the Multifamily Zoning Law.  
This law requires that each of the 177 MBTA communities in Eastern Massachusetts designate an 
area or areas in their community where multi-family housing is allowed by-right. The Town must 
designate at least 50 acres where multi-family housing is allowed by-right and allow a housing density 
of 15 units per acre in the designated area.  There cannot be age or other restrictions that would 
make units unsuitable for families with children. Failure to comply with this law would lead to severe 
financial consequences including the loss of future state grant opportunities as well as possibly being 
sued by the Massachusetts Office of the Attorney General.  
Led by the Bedford Planning Board, the Town is working diligently to come into compliance with the 
new law.  This process included a number a public meetings, surveys and public hearings.  The 
Planning Board is now finishing the selection of two housing overlay districts. One centers on the 
Depot Park/Railroad Ave area continuing down Loomis Street to a section of Great Road. The second 
overlay district called Shawsheen West covers the commercial area of Alfred Circle and Roberts Drive 
to Great Road. Together the two districts total 55 acres and have a unit capacity of 759 units.  
Why  
There has been a shortage of housing construction for the last many decades in the Commonwealth 
which is a major reason housing costs keep increasing. The problem compounds itself which is why 
the median prices for a standard single-family home has increased in Bedford from $375,000 in 2000 
to $949,950 in 2022 or 153% and from $698,000 in 2017 or an increase of 36% in just 5 years.1 
The fact remains that Eastern Massachusetts of which Bedford is a part, has been and remains in a 
housing crisis.  Not only are housing of all types becoming more expensive, there is not enough 
housing of all types being built, particularly affordable housing. 
• 31% of Bedford renters are cost-burdened meaning they are paying more than 30% of their income 
toward rent.2

[PAGE 2]
• Fewer than 50% of household can afford the median rent for 2 or 3BR units in 2022 without being 
cost burdened. In 2023 an income of $142,800 is needed to afford the median 2BR rent of $3,570 a 
month and $160,000 is needed to afford the median 3BR rent of $4,000 a month.3  
• The burden is even greater on Seniors. 30% of households headed by someone 65+ have an income 
of $50,000 or less while only 8% of households headed by someone under 65 years of age have an 
income of $50,000 or less.4 
• Buying a house is too expensive for many current residents.  Median sales price of a house in 2022 is 
$949,950.5  A household would need an income of $248,306 to afford this.  Yet the median income in 
Bedford is $140,467 which affords a home of $504,700.6 
• Massachusetts ranks 42nd out of 50 in the number of housing permits approved per capita. This is half 
of the average housing permit rate.7 
Benefits 
There are a number of benefits that will come from complying with this law allowing multi-family 
housing by-right. These include:  
• Provide people who are employed in Bedford or who grew up in Bedford greater opportunities to live 
in town including our public-school teachers and municipal workers.  Greater densities and smaller 
units are expected to be lower in cost. 
• Provide downsizing options for seniors. 
• Support first time homebuyers and young families. 
• Provide additional affordable housing opportunities, and broader missing middle housing designs. 
Ultimately, the goal is to provide adequate housing that is inclusive of all household types.

• Requirement per Massachusetts General Law, Chapter 40A, Section 3A
• Applies to communities with MBTA service and ones adjacent to those that have it
• Zoning requirement not a housing production requirement
• MBTA Communities (which include Bedford) must designate an area 
where multi-family housing is allowed as-of-right.  Requirements include:  
• Land area at least 50 acres
• Allow at least 15 units per acre
• No age restrictions and suitable for families with children
• If multiple areas, one area must be >50% of total district area
• Bedford chooses the location of the zone, subject to State approval
• Proposed Bylaw meets requirements of MGL, Ch. 40A, Section 3A
Executive Summary

[PAGE 5]
• 177 “MBTA Communities” – essentially all 
of eastern MA
• Specific requirements depend upon level 
of MBTA service.  Four types:
• Rapid transit
• Commuter rail
• Adjacent Community
• Adjacent Small Town
• Total multifamily units MBTA 
Communities must zone for under the 
law:  296,803
• Multifamily units Bedford must zone for 
under the law:  750 (0.25% of total)

• Multifamily housing

amenity structures

The purposes of the Multifamily Housing Overlay District (MFHOD) 
are to:
• Allow creation of housing as-of-right in a variety of sizes and types;
• Reduce dependency on automobiles by providing opportunities for multifamily 
housing near public transportation, bikeway networks, shopping, and local 
services;
• Preserve open space by allowing new multifamily housing within or adjacent 
to existing developed areas and infrastructure;
• Encourage economic investment in the redevelopment of properties; 
• Expand the customer base for local businesses; and
• Ensure compliance with MGL c. 40A § 3A.
