# A quorum its own record outvotes is not a ballot count

Ashburnham 2006's page prints, above the results,

    Total Registered Voters = 3,733
    Total Votes Cast = 683
    Percentage of Registered Votes that Voted = 18.3%

683 ballots. The ballot count derived from the record was 534, and on the
strength of it three contests were reported as **arithmetically impossible** --
Moderator 583, One Board of Selectmen 683, One Municipal Light Board 542. Every
one of those figures is printed on the page exactly as the record holds it.

534 came from two Planning Board races that tallied 534 each. Nothing else in
the record agreed with them, and five contests implied more. The derivation
took the modal quotient and never asked whether the value it picked was
possible.

`marks <= ballots x seats` holds for every contest in a record. So a contest
implying MORE ballots than a candidate value is evidence against that value, not
a defect in itself. Two contests agreeing is the minimum; being outnumbered by
the contests you contradict is disqualifying. Where that happens the honest
answer is that the record cannot say how many ballots were cast.

That silence is not the one 294 published records already carry. Nothing
closing exactly is a gap in what can be CHECKED. A quorum its own record
outvotes means a figure or a seat count somewhere in that record is wrong and
the arithmetic cannot say which -- a finding, and worded differently so the gate
can keep it out of publish.

24 records were affected and 192 contests stopped being called impossible.
Two of them show why "cannot derive" is the right answer rather than a better
number:

- **Cummington 2018** tallies 181, 166, 156, 150, 162, 153, 161, 150, 155, 161,
  152, 158, 149, 142 across twenty contests. The clerk prints no Blanks line, so
  no contest closes and every total is different. Two happened to be 150. The
  record is perfect.
- **Carver 2000** prints Moderator three times, at 326, 457 and 562 -- one
  column per precinct, read as three town-wide contests. Those sum to 1,345,
  which is what the page prints as "Total Votes cast".

A rival count carrying its own quorum is the same problem from the other side.
Falmouth 2017 closes five office races at exactly 7,243 and all four of its
ballot questions at exactly 7,244. The town's two tallies differ by one ballot;
neither block is the impossible one, and calling the five right condemns the
four.
