# A table of contents is a heading and a list of offices

317 of 1,331 pre-2021 parses found no election, because the cut held none. 89
of those cuts are an index, a contents page or an officers directory: a heading,
office vocabulary, and not one figure. That is exactly the shape the locator
scored highest, because it counted headings and offices and treated figures as a
bonus.

Two costs, one already paid.

**Avon 2013.** The cut is the report's Table of Contents, page 9. The return is
on pages 50 to 52 and reads

    BOARD OF HEALTH: ............................................... 3 years
    vote for one
    Robert A. Ogilvie, 28 Butler Ave ...............................302
    Write In: .........................................................0
     Blanks:  .........................................................91

Dot leaders, which is the shape that hid Topsfield's whole run for twelve years,
and this time hid the page rather than the line.

**Carver 2014.** The cut is a financial statement, the index -- numbered 111,
reading "Elections: / Annual Town Election Results, 4/26/14 .... 10 / State
Election Results, 11/4/14 .... 19" -- and a blank page. Nine contests, thirteen
named candidates and twenty-two vote totals were read off it and published. Not
one of those names or figures is printed anywhere in the section.

A return carries FIGURES against names and an index does not. A page already
showing ballot vocabulary is scored on how many of its lines put a label against
a number, and on whether it heads itself the ANNUAL election rather than the
state one printed forty pages later.

Both terms have to be gated on that ballot vocabulary, and the gate is the part
that took the measuring:

- ungated, the figures promoted a police department's three-year statistics
  table over Boxborough 2015's return;
- ungated, the word ANNUAL promoted the contents line "Annual Town Election
  Results .... 10" over the election itself;
- capping the office term -- the other obvious way to stop an index winning --
  moved Mashpee 2014 OFF its return, because a real return names more offices
  than an index does. The office count was never what separated them.

Measured on ten reports whose cut found nothing and eleven whose cut is
published: four of the ten move to the return, ten of the eleven are unchanged
and one moves by a single page, inside the window added either way.
